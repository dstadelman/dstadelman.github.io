# Act 1 — The World As It Is
## Lecture 1: History and Understanding

Dramatic question: what is this thing I live inside?
Final image: standing at the shore.
Runtime: 60 minutes, ~25 slides.

Internal three acts:
- Setup (0–10): the naive lens — orgs explained by good and bad people.
- Turn (10–45): history and theory break the naive lens.
- Resolution (45–60): this is not cynicism, it is relief. The org is weather.

---

## Beat outline

### Cold open — the coffin-maker (5 min, slides 1–3)
1. Title slide: "The Corporation as a Force of Nature — Part 1: The World As It Is."
2. The Han Feizi line, alone on a slide: "the carriage-maker wishes people to be rich and noble; the coffin-maker wishes people to die young. It is not that the carriage-maker is benevolent and the coffin-maker is cruel" — "his profit lies in people's deaths" ([Hanfeizi ch. 17, warringstates.day translation](https://warringstates.day/library/hanfeizi/17-bei-nei)). Han Feizi, ch. 17, ca. 233 BCE.
3. Context beat — two explanations on one slide:
   - The villain view (common, and not helpful): "a few bad apples ruined a good org." It feels right, and it leaves you nothing to do but be angry or be a victim.
   - The structural view: the coffin-maker isn't cruel; his position pays him when people die. Positions explain behavior better than hearts.
   - Commit to the structural lens for the series.
   - Speaker notes: the bad-apple reading is the lens the audience walked in with; we name it so we can set it down. It is not the thesis.

### Part 1 — The corporation was never designed (15 min, slides 4–9)
4. English East India Company 1600, Dutch VOC 1602 — corporations as charters, not blueprints. They are usually credited with the "textbook" features: permanent capital, legal personhood, separation of ownership and management, limited liability, tradable shares — but the VOC got them one by one, and the EIC only in the 1650s (Gelderblom, De Jong & Jonker 2013).
5. The patchwork history (Gelderblom, De Jong, Jonker): permanent capital arrived 1612, director limited liability 1623 — each feature patched in to fix a crisis — "piecemeal engineering to remedy design flaws," driven by "severe cash constraints resulting from its military and commercial operations." Transferable shares and shareholder limited liability existed from the start; "the five features did not come as a package." "Legal form followed economic function, not the other way around." (Gelderblom, De Jong & Jonker, JEH 2013, [paper](https://dspace.library.uu.nl/server/api/core/bitstreams/40233814-d2c9-4c5d-bf08-995db6f11931/content))
6. Point slide: structures evolve under pressure — usually pressure about capital and control, never about human goodness.
7. Alfred Chandler, The Visible Hand: salaried managers replace owners; separation of ownership and control.
8. The M-form (multi-divisional structure) — Chandler, Strategy and Structure (1962), not The Visible Hand: a "general office plans, coordinates, and appraises the work of a number of operating divisions and allocates to them the necessary personnel, facilities, funds and other resources" (p. 2). Du Pont, General Motors, Standard Oil (NJ) and Sears each began this reorganization in the 1920s, independently of one another (p. 3). His thesis: structure follows strategy (p. 14). Divisions as internal nations. (Page cites via [Strategy and Structure summary](https://www.scribd.com/document/55362038/Chandler-Strategy-and-Structure-Summ1); p. 14 also cited at [bizsci.net](https://handbook.bizsci.net/macro/).)
9. The ownership/control gap is where corporate politics lives. The second lens we will question — the cynic's version: "nobody runs the machine for the reasons the machine claims." It sounds like clarity, but it is just the bad-apple view scaled up: instead of a few bad apples, everyone is lying. Chandler's story is calmer: owners and managers are in different positions, so they want different things — weather, not a conspiracy.
   - Speaker notes: let the audience feel the pull of the cynical reading, then step past it. The gap is real; the motive-hunting is not useful. Positions explain behavior better than hidden bad intent.

### Part 2 — Why hierarchy always re-forms (15 min, slides 10–15)
10. Robert Michels 1911, Political Parties: "Who says organization, says oligarchy." ([Michels, Political Parties, McMaster text](https://socserv.socsci.mcmaster.ca/~econ/ugcm/3ll3/michels/polipart.pdf)) His recurring case is the German socialist party — a modern democratic party (e.g., "Accumulation of functions is, in fact, one of the characteristics of modern democratic parties. In the German socialist party we not infrequently find…," same text, Part 2). The iron law: delegation creates specialists, specialists entrench. Even orgs founded to prevent hierarchy become hierarchical.
11. Max Weber: bureaucracy as rule by files, by rules, by position. Economy and Society: "The management of the modern office is based upon written documents ('the files')"; "The management of the office follows general rules, which are more or less stable…"; and a "firmly ordered system of super- and subordination" ([Weber, "Bureaucracy," Wirtschaft und Gesellschaft pt. III ch. 6](https://www.d.umn.edu/cla/faculty/jhamlin/4111/Weber_Lec_Readings/Weber%20-%20Bureaucracy.htm)). The "iron cage" is a different book: Parsons's 1930 translation of stahlhartes Gehäuse ("a shell as hard as steel") at the end of The Protestant Ethic ([Baehr 2001](https://psi412.cankaya.edu.tr/uploads/files/Baehr,%20The%20Iron%20Cage%20(2001).pdf)). [Corrected attribution.]
12. Han Feizi again, from the Chinese side: don't trust the minister's heart, read the position he stands in. Ministers serve because "the constraint of political circumstance… compels service," not kinship ([Hanfeizi ch. 17, warringstates.day translation](https://warringstates.day/library/hanfeizi/17-bei-nei)). The coffin-maker from slide 2 is his point in one image. The oldest structuralist in the room.
13. Conway's Law (Melvin Conway, 1968, Datamation): "organizations which design systems ... are constrained to produce designs which are copies of the communication structures of these organizations." His own example: eight people, five on COBOL and three on ALGOL — the COBOL compiler ran in five phases, the ALGOL in three. "Two military services ... produced a copy of their organization chart." ([Conway, How Do Committees Invent?](https://www.melconway.com/Home/Committees_Paper.html)) The coffin-maker in engineering terms — structure doesn't just shape what people want, it determines what they can produce.
14. Conway is testable in a way Michels never was. Nagappan, Murphy & Basili (Microsoft Research, 2008): on Windows Vista, organizational-structure metrics predicted failure-prone binaries better than churn, complexity or coverage ([TR-2008-11](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/tr-2008-11.pdf)). Long, Markovtsev et al. (2019) restored GitLab's engineering org "in broad strokes" from commit activity alone across 117 projects ([arXiv 1905.06782](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.06782)). The thing you shipped is a fossil record of the org. [Corrected: "Vaish, Gajos & Manku (2017)" could not be found; replaced with these two verified studies.]
15. Synthesis slide: four traditions, one finding — hierarchy re-forms because that is what the structure selects for.

### Part 3 — Structure as an X-ray of values (12 min, slides 16–20)
16. How to read what an org actually believes: what gets measured, what gets promoted, how units are split, where budget authority sits. Start with what a company files about itself: open a large company's annual report and read its reportable segments.
17. Example: a company split into competing reportable segments believes internal competition produces accountability.
18. The human-nature answer to why we stay: Takeo Doi's amae (甘え) — "to depend and presume upon another's benevolence," used for a child toward a parent, "but it can also be used to describe the relationship between two adults, such as… a master and a subordinate." Doi: "there is no single word in English equivalent to amaeru, though this does not mean that the psychology of amae is totally alien to the people of English-speaking countries." Japanese has a whole family of words for it — suneru (pouts and sulks because he feels he is not allowed to amaeru), tereru (ashamed of showing his intimate wish to amaeru), hinekureru (takes devious ways in his efforts to deny the wish to amaeru) ([Doi 1962, "Amae: A Key Concept for Understanding Japanese Personality Structure"](https://doi.org/10.2117/psysoc.1962.1); expanded in The Anatomy of Dependence). The feeling is universal; Japan named it. The corporation is a modern answer to amae: it offers security and belonging, and asks for loyalty in return. [Corrected: removed "Japan is the only culture…" — Doi says no English equivalent, not that no other culture has one.]
19. Ruth Benedict's U-curve (The Chrysanthemum and the Sword, 1946, ch. 12 "The Child Learns"): "The arc of life in Japan is plotted in opposite fashion to that in the United States. It is a great shallow U-curve with maximum freedom and indulgence allowed to babies and to the old." The low "continues many years during the prime of life"; "In the United States we stand this curve upside down" ([ch. 12 text](https://navymule9.sakura.ne.jp/Benedict1946-12.html)). The org sits in that restricted middle. Naming it takes the resentment out of it. [Corrected: removed "quoted by Doi" — not verified.]
20. Exercise prompt: your org's X-ray — three questions to ask this week.

### Part 4 — The emotional turn (10 min, slides 21–24)
21. The objection: isn't this bleak? Isn't this giving up?
22. The turn: it isn't cynicism, it's relief. Nothing feels like betrayal once you read structure. A wave isn't malicious. A landslide isn't personal. You stop being sad at the mountain for being a mountain.
23. Marcus Aurelius 4.3 (George Long translation): "nowhere either with more quiet or more freedom from trouble does a man retire than into his own soul." The calm isn't escaping to the mountain; it's the disposition you bring to the meeting room. And 5.20: "the mind converts and changes every hindrance to its activity into an aid; and so that which is a hindrance is made a furtherance to an act; and that which is an obstacle on the road helps us on this road" ([Long translation, Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/15877)). You can't fight the organization, and you don't have to be sad about it. You just gotta go. [Corrected: replaced unverified paraphrase wording with Long's text.]
24. Bridge to Act 2: next time, we learn to read the specific terrain — and we'll borrow a culture that names out loud what ours hides.

### Close (3 min, slide 25)
25. The coffin-maker again — now earned: "It is not that the carriage-maker is benevolent and the coffin-maker is cruel" ([Hanfeizi ch. 17, warringstates.day translation](https://warringstates.day/library/hanfeizi/17-bei-nei)). You came in looking for villains; you leave reading positions. Homework: pick one decision at your company you believe happened in a meeting. Find where it actually happened.
