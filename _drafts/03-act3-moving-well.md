# Act 3 — Moving Well
## Lecture 3: Building and Exercising Power

Dramatic question: given all this, how do I act — and how do I get more power while doing it?
Final image: the sailor.
Runtime: 60 minutes, ~27 slides.

Internal three acts:
- Setup (0–12): the operating stance — how to hold all of this without going bitter.
- Turn (12–44): navigation tactics — the concrete moves that build power.
- Resolution (44–60): the choice of which fights to fight, and the Annie quote from the other side.

---

## Beat outline

### Cold open — the sailor (5 min, slides 1–3)
1. Title slide: "Part 3: Moving Well."
2. Image: a sailor. Not fighting the ocean, not sad about the ocean — reading it, trimmed to it, going somewhere.
3. Thesis: Act 1 gave you sight, Act 2 gave you a map. This hour is about the sailing. Navigation, not manipulation.

### Part 1 — The operating stance (12 min, slides 4–9)
4. Marcus Aurelius 2.1 — the morning preparation: expect the meddling and the ungrateful; we were made to cooperate like hands and eyelids. Not bitterness. A plan for the day.
5. The dichotomy of control: the structure is not yours; your conduct is. Invest only where you have agency.
6. Amor fati — not merely bearing the org, but using it. 5.20: the impediment to action advances action. What stands in the way becomes the way.
7. Why this stance is the precondition for power: reactive people get used as weather vanes. Calm people get consulted. The org routes around the emotional and defers to the steady.
8. The emotional payoff restated: you said it yourself — this changed how you enjoy your job. You're not sad anymore because you're dealing with something like the ocean or the mountain.
9. Bridge: stance without tactics is just therapy. Now the moves.

### Part 2 — Navigation: how power is actually built (27 min, slides 10–20)
10. Pfeffer's frame: power is a skill set, not a personality trait. Learnable, practiced, deniable if you're good at it.
11. Move 1 — sponsorship. A sponsor isn't a mentor; a sponsor spends their own capital on you in rooms you're not in. How to make yourself sponsorable: do visible work that makes your sponsor look smart.
12. Move 2 — visibility. Competence that nobody knows about is functionally incompetence. Manage the narrative of your work without self-promotion theater — reports, summaries, the pre-meeting.
13. Move 3 — resource control. Budget, headcount, information, and unique knowledge are the four currencies. Hold at least one that nobody else holds.
14. Move 4 — brokerage. Stand in the structural holes (Burt): connect the clusters that don't talk. Brokers see the nemawashi coming. Brokers get asked.
15. Move 5 — do the pre-meeting. Now you do nemawashi yourself: decisions are won one-on-one before the meeting. This is the Act 2 map turned into motion.
16. Move 6 — pick battles the org can absorb. Not every wrong is yours to fight. The Survey Corps survived because its leaders were good at funding and optics, not because they were right.
17. Move 7 — the inverse Conway maneuver. Conway's Law says the product copies the org's communication structure; run it backward and you get the most senior move available — redraw the seams between teams and you change what the org is capable of producing. Framing matters: you're not manipulating people, you're changing the terrain. Only people with real standing get to redraw seams, which makes this both a power move and a measure of how much power you have.
18. What this looks like over 12 months: a concrete sequence — map the terrain, hold one resource, broker one cross-unit thing, earn one sponsor, win one pre-meeting.
19. The power question answered directly: this is how you get more power. Not by out-lobbying the politicians — by being the person the machine structurally needs.
20. The costs, honestly: these moves take energy and patience. Some of them feel fake at first. They stop feeling fake when you realize you're just being legible to a system that was never legible to you.

### Part 3 — The choice (13 min, slides 21–26)
21. The objection, finally: "if it's all structure, why bother being good?" Because the informal org runs on trust, and trust is the one resource you can't buy.
22. Navigation vs. manipulation, drawn plainly: manipulation uses the map to surprise people; navigation uses the map to arrive where decisions happen and be useful there. One burns trust, the other compounds it.
23. The AoT counter-example: Eren fights the institution directly; every attempt costs the people around him. The survivors are the structural readers — Hange with patronage, Levi choosing battles, Armin reading what the enemy's structure will and won't do.
24. You don't have to love the machine. You don't have to fix it. You have to know it and move well through it. That's enough. That's the whole series.
25. The Annie quote, one final time — now from the other side. She wasn't the sad one in that scene. She was the only one who wasn't deluded. The clarity is what set her free.
26. Series close: victim of the machine → reader of the machine → navigator of it. Final image: the sailor, trimmed to the current, going somewhere.

### Close (2 min, slide 27)
27. One homework for the whole series: name one decision you'll win by pre-meeting this quarter. Tell someone in the room which one.
