# Summary: What to Use the Latest AI Tools For

[The AI Daily Brief - What to Use the Latest AI Tools For](https://podcasters.spotify.com/pod/show/nlw/episodes/What-to-Use-the-Latest-AI-Tools-For-e3onnu3)

NLW's second episode of a two-episode week opens with the day's big news -- Anthropic's misuse report, Sam Altman's first signal of openness to slowing down, and OpenAI pausing its $200 Pro plan -- then pivots to the practical: roughly nine product launches from the last couple of days, each with a simple frame of who should use it and what to do with it. The through-line is that this release wave says more about where things are headed than what it ships today: voice is becoming the primary interface to computers, and model stacks are being optimized for good-enough capability at low cost rather than pure frontier performance.

* **Anthropic's new misuse report**: Anthropic says it disrupted distillation attacks from Alibaba, DeepSeek, and Xiaomi, all of which used networks of fraudulent accounts to extract reasoning traces from Claude models for training data; the report was curated to highlight the most notable and novel threats, and says the risks of increasingly capable models will grow unless developers and defenders act.
* **Moonshot allegedly relayed 300,000 requests to Claude**: Anthropic claims DeepSeek and Moonshot's Kimi K3 routed customer requests to Claude and served the responses to their own users -- over a 10-day period Moonshot relayed almost 300,000 requests, mostly to Opus -- apparently to gather realistic queries for a distillation pipeline, which exposed sensitive Chinese government and corporate data on multiple occasions.
* **A bio-safety block**: Anthropic's classifier blocked a grant application to identify enhancing mutations of the chikungunya virus, engineer them into infectious clones, and select for virulence in live animals -- flagged as potentially malicious because the work was intended for a military research institute, even though it could have had innocent vaccine-development applications.
* **Altman says he's open to an AI slowdown**: at an all-hands, Sam Altman said OpenAI could pace the development of new models, likely in coordination with other labs, following ex-Anthropic Jacob Coxon's viral extinction warning, chief scientist Jakub Pachocki's call for "voluntary slowdowns," and the addition of safety researcher Paul Christiano to OpenAI's board -- unlike his counterpart Dario Amodei, Altman had not signed the "Pacing the Frontier" open letter until now.
* **OpenAI hits a compute wall**: after GPT-6 Astra's release drove "unprecedented" demand, OpenAI paused new $200 Pro subscriptions as "the smallest step" to protect existing users; all other plans and the API remain available, and as one commentator put it, "the era of subsidized tokens is ending."
* **Microsoft plans to triple data-center capacity**: the company now targets 38 gigawatts of global capacity by 2032, up from 12 today, with AI's share growing to a third of total compute and CPUs added for agentic workflows -- evidence, per NLW, that AI infrastructure is nowhere near overbuilt and that hyperscalers expect at least five more years of elevated CapEx.
* **Jensen Huang doubles down on 70% growth**: speaking at a Goldman Sachs conference, the NVIDIA CEO said demand is much greater than 70% but supply lets him confidently deliver 70%, that a single NVLink-connected GPU system costs $8.5 million, and that rack-scale orders are growing 27% month-over-month -- the first time in a year he's discussed forward estimates.
* **DOJ is investigating the Groq deal**: the probe looks at whether NVIDIA's $20 billion non-exclusive licensing deal -- which also brought Groq CEO Jonathan Ross and COO Sunny Madra to NVIDIA -- was structured to circumvent antitrust review, joining a wave of "not-acquisitions" (Google/Character, Google/Windsurf, Meta/Scale) that senators have accused of functioning as de facto mergers.
* **Meta's Muse agent lands on Wall Street**: Muse drew 83,000 iOS downloads on launch day -- modest for Meta (Threads did 4.3 million) but enough for second place in the App Store -- and the stock jumped 6% on release day, with JP Morgan upgrading to buy on the theory that Meta's ~4 billion users are a decisive distribution advantage.
* **GPT Live 1 is now in the API**: the full-duplex voice model that talks and listens at the same time, handles noisy environments and interruptions, and hands tasks off to a backend reasoning model is now priced at $0.05 per minute for audio plus standard API pricing for the backend -- NLW calls it a hacker's dream and says it lets you build your own anywhere personal assistant on a cheap dev screen and mic.
* **Devin Voice**: Cognition's "your favorite AI software engineer just got a landline" launch pairs GPT Live 1 on the voice layer with the new SWE-2 model, and Nader Dabit of Cognition says 90% of his day-to-day work is now done by voice -- a big quality-of-life upgrade over turn-based prompting.
* **Cognition SWE-2 shows how far "good enough" has climbed**: a post-trained version of Kimi K3 optimized purely for coding, it scored 50% on Frontier Code 1.1 -- slightly ahead of GPT-5.6 Sol and just behind Fable 5.1 -- at a 64% cost reduction and even cheaper than SWE-1.7, with effort levels to dial usage down further; it lands as Cognition raises $2 billion at a $48 billion valuation and signals it wants to stay independent.
* **DeepSeek V4.1 Flash**: a 552-billion-parameter model priced at the bottom of the market ($0.30 per million input tokens, $1.20 per million output) that, per Artificial Analysis, outperforms DeepSeek's full-size Pro model on the intelligence index at roughly a quarter of the cost -- a fit for teams with basic but highly recurring, compute-heavy tasks building a holistic model architecture.
* **OpenAI's small business plugin collection**: a one-stop bundle of Dropbox, HubSpot, Canva, Figma, Shopify, DocuSign, PayPal, QuickBooks, Stripe, Gusto, Slack, and other connectors for SMB owners using ChatGPT -- though as one commenter noted, the unsolved part is distribution, since SMB owners don't browse plugin directories.
* **ChatGPT for Finance gets the GPT-6 Astra treatment**: now living inside ChatGPT Work with built-in access to premium data feeds (DeLupa, PitchBook, LSEG News, Crunchbase), enterprise governance controls, and a custom charting experience and SEC filing viewer, redesigned with Morgan Stanley and Evercore for financial models, research, and client materials.
* **A power tool, not a replacement**: NLW argues senior bankers won't build their own models and decks just because agents got better at that core work -- the real signal is that firms like UBS now require prospective junior bankers to demonstrate AI proficiency as a hiring requirement.
* **A new data agent for ChatGPT Work**: connects to Redshift, Datadog, BigQuery, ClickHouse, Databricks, MongoDB, and Snowflake to turn proprietary data into insights, with the ability to publish new views directly to Tableau, where Southard Jones, chief product officer at Tableau, framed it as bringing trusted business semantics to where employees already work.
* **Verticalization is an industry pattern**: xAI's Grok Bot announced native integrations with Salesforce, HubSpot, Gong, Clay, Granola, and other go-to-market tools for sales teams -- the same tool-access-plus-context-management trend as OpenAI's business pushes.
* **Cursor Projects turns a folder into a project architecture**: beyond accumulating context, the projects tab houses scheduled tasks, long-running agents, and a persistent coordinator thread that plans and delegates to subagents; testers merged six times as many PRs, with merge rates up 30%.
* **The monothread era**: compaction in Claude and Codex has gotten good enough that one long thread can build, deploy, and keep working across repos without context handoffs -- NLW runs the entire AI Daily Brief website through a single long-running thread that has never hit a context wall.

The meta-point of the episode: a launch matters both for what you can build with it today and for where it says everyone is headed. Voice is the clearest bet -- NLW points to Whisper Flow and ChatGPT's native voice as cheap ways to shift your own workflow over now, since the industry is pushing you there whether you opt in or not -- while the stack of cheaper, task-tuned models and verticalized agents quietly makes serious work dramatically less expensive to run.
