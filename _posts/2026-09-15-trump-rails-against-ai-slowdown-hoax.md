# Summary: Trump Rails Against AI Slowdown "Hoax"

[AI Daily Brief - Trump Rails Against AI Slowdown "Hoax"](https://podcasters.spotify.com/pod/show/nlw/episodes/Trump-Rails-Against-AI-Slowdown-Hoax-e3ot4h6)

Nathaniel Whittemore reports that Dario Amodei's "pacing the frontier" proposal -- backed by other lab leaders -- was supposed to open a negotiation over who controls the speed of AI, but politics swallowed the technical discussion within 48 hours. President Trump fired off seven Truth Social posts calling AI doom a "hoax" and a "SICK conspiracy," Obama and Kamala Harris each publicly backed the slowdown, and Jensen Huang got a live Oval Office call from the president while on stage at the All-In Summit. The episode also covers the fallout from Terence Tao and 25 Fields medalists signing an open letter against AI companies solving Millennium Prize problems as benchmarks, a Census Bureau study documenting a sharp hit to AI-exposed college graduates, and Zhipu AI's (Z.ai) $5 billion raise earmarked for a "fully self-training" loop.

* **Trump's Truth Social blitz**: Posted seven times over the course of the day, declaring that the only "guardrail" AI needs is a "STRONG AND SMART (High IQ!) PRESIDENT," that there is a "SICK conspiracy going on against AI and Data Centers, and the only one that is happy about it is China," that "AI taking over the World, destroying Humanity, and all other things bad, is a HOAX," and that he is "the Hoax Buster" breaking "another Hoax... even wilder than the RUSSIA, RUSSIA, RUSSIA HOAX."
* **Partisan framing lands on both sides**: Obama publicly endorsed the slowdown at a Democratic fundraising event, saying AI would be "one of my central agendas" if he were running for president in 2028, and Kamala Harris chimed in on X asking Congress for a new federal AI regulator and a treaty with China on dangerous uses. Whittemore notes the AI safety community watched its "nightmare scenario" unfold -- AI safety became a tribal issue where half the country opposes it because the other half supports it.
* **Terence Tao and 25 Fields medalists**: Signed "A Severe Misalignment of AI in Mathematics," arguing that the labs' rush to solve Millennium Prize problems (Navier-Stokes is one) as benchmarks "is detrimental to the science of mathematics," and that "sol[ving] problems is only a tool and proxy for achieving the primary goal of conceptual understanding and insight." Several signatories, including Tao, acknowledged AI is genuinely useful for research while warning against "a general threat to intellectual work" across scientific and creative fields.
* **NYU's Tristan Buckmaster**: Questioned whether OpenAI accessed his research logs before claiming the Navier-Stokes prize, and the company denied doing so -- part of a wider dispute that already includes OpenAI, Anthropic, Tao (who publicly assessed Buckmaster's work), and other math figures. Three of the six remaining Millennium Prize problems could plausibly fall to AI this year.
* **Census Bureau study**: "Graduating into Disruption" finds that the most AI-exposed decile of college majors, since ChatGPT's launch in late 2022, saw a 5-percentage-point drop in initial employment and a 13% drop in initial earnings -- "comparable in magnitude to the earnings losses associated with graduating into a large recession" -- partly because they shifted into lower-paid restaurants and retail. Erik Brynjolfsson's Stanford "Canaries in the Coal Mine" work is the closest analogue, though causation is still contested.
* **Z.ai (Zhipu AI) raises $5B**: Announced Sunday on the Hong Kong exchange, split roughly $2B in a share placement and $3B in zero-coupon convertible bonds, with ~60% earmarked for next-generation GLM models and what the company calls a "fully self-training loop" -- the first time a Chinese lab has openly committed to recursive self-improvement as a funded research goal.
* **"The Last AI Built by Humans"**: A 33-researcher paper with ByteDance backing (along with ModelBest, Xiaohongshu, Shanghai AI Lab and others) published the same week mapping a five-stage roadmap to recursive self-improvement, concluding that AI can automate parts of training today but there is "no evidence" it can yet improve the process of self-improvement itself.
* **Jensen Huang's All-In Summit interview**: Chamath Palihapitiya asked how to explain a >10% extinction risk to the public, and Huang pushed back: "We shouldn't, because it's made up... I would bet you money that in every one of those cases, [safety incidents] are within their control in the future to prevent." He endorsed third-party evaluators embedded in labs (the core of Amodei's own proposal), then took President Trump's live call from the Oval Office and put it on speakerphone, saying "we're not going to let it happen, sir."
* **JD Vance and the War Department**: Vance said at Andrews Air Force Base that frontier labs "coming to the government and begging the government to regulate them" feels "a bit of a Trojan horse," and the Department of War CTO account posted a "AI First" / "Americanism, not effective altruism" graphic.
* **Market reaction**: The NASDAQ was flat on Monday, but the semiconductor index closed down 5.9% on the slowdown rhetoric, and a few commentators on X pointed to a possible short-term pivot toward SaaS or even "much-maligned Indian IT stocks" as places to hide while the AI sector takes a tumble.

With the lines drawn, the debate has officially entered its political phase: whether the slowdown question becomes a durable policy fight or a tribal slogan, and whether the technical work -- embedded evaluators, verifiable self-training, international coordination -- survives long enough to become the actual agenda at the 2026 midterms.
