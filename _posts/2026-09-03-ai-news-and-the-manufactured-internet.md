# Summary: AI News And The Manufactured Internet

[Plausibly Deniable - AI News And The Manufactured Internet | Brian Chau](https://open.spotify.com/episode/4gyXofHTgwXxrvLaKw0h29)

In this Plausibly Deniable episode, host Lucas sits down with Brian Chau -- a former machine-learning engineer, author of the "From the New World" Substack, a one-time DC pro-AI lobbyist, and now the operator of Effort.New, a venture that runs long-context AI queries to surface under-reported stories. The conversation covers how modern LLM writing has degraded, how AI can be turned on government data to break real stories, and a long critique of what the hosts call the "manufactured Internet" -- protests, NGOs, and media narratives they argue are privately funded and artificially manufactured.

* **Effort.New in a nutshell**: Brian describes the company as doing the research with AI but keeping the writing human, because "AI writing is somewhat grating and painful." The team runs long-context queries over federal, financial, and legal documents that almost nobody reads, and says it has "accelerated so fast" in two months of operation that it expects to hold a durable lead.
* **A headline story -- religious orgs and federal grants**: The pair recount a viral Effort story about Christian/religious organizations that are repeatedly trotted out on left-wing media to claim to "speak for Christians" on open borders and refugee services, and which (per the episode) pull down nine-figure HHS grant dollars -- roughly $162 million -- for migrant and refugee services, with the funding itself coming from the Office of Refugee Resettlement and HHS grant tables, all of which sat in a public federal database for years before being surfaced. [The specific $162M figure and the Office of Refugee Resettlement tie-in are the host's claims and could not be independently confirmed in this verification pass.]
* **How the story broke**: Brian says he made an offline copy of the HHS grant table, pointed a long-context model at it, and asked it to surface anything newsworthy -- funding jumps in particular -- and the top hit was this one. He called journalist friends to ask if anyone had covered it; nobody had.
* **AI writing has "descended down a slope"**: Brian argues that frontier-model writing style is getting *worse* as post-training gets more refined, and traces part of the blame to RLVR (reinforcement-learning from verifiable rewards) and to models training models, which optimizes for efficient token communication between sub-agents rather than for producing prose a human reader enjoys. He calls out Claude as especially sycophantic and patronizing, and recalls early GPT-3 "schizophrenic breaks" and Gemini context leaks as producing some of the most interesting writing anyone has seen from an LLM.
* **Sycophancy is the core failure mode**: Brian says he would rather be told "you cannot do that" than told "you're the smartest person ever for the millionth time," and notes that even OpenAI's public chain-of-thought traces are not the model's actual traces -- they're summaries deliberately kept short to prevent distillation.
* **Models are picky about sources**: A running theme is that different labs optimize their search and retrieval behavior very differently. GLM-family (5.x) models, in the hosts' experience, lean into anecdotal and forum sources; OpenAI models lean into academic sources. Both are pushed by benchmarks that are adversarially designed against the raw web, so a wrong forum answer or an old Wikipedia number will cost model scores and the labs tune around it.
* **Data-center protests and the "manufactured Internet"**: The hosts argue that the data-center protest surge is coordinated and financed -- in the episode, by a "China-connected billionaire" [narrated in the transcript as "Neville Singham," who is under investigation per the episode; the name and status could not be independently verified]. They add that college-student recruits are paid roughly $200 a day plus transport, and that the protests have "fizzled out" three or four previous cycles -- deepfakes, energy use, and now data centers -- each time.
* **Anti-AI NGOs**: The pair say a lot of the lower-tier "anti-AI" and "anti-datacenter" NGOs are effectively owned by a small number of mega-donors -- in this episode, Dustin Moskovitz, Sam Bankman-Fried, and a third [phonetically garbled in the transcript] -- with the result that the NGOs are unaligned to their funders and simply "pop into existence and then immediately evaporate."
* **California SB 1047 and the UHW union**: A long thread is that the UHW union used the billionaires-tax bill and SB 1047 (which the episode says would have effectively banned GPT-5-class models) as leverage to extract concessions over a separate healthcare bill, and that the union "goes about without any retaliation for all of their mafia tactics for more than 20 years." [SB 1047 / union-leverage specifics could not be independently confirmed in this pass.]
* **Nick Shirley and the "Stop Nick Shirley" law**: Brian discusses YouTuber Nick Shirley's fraud-counter approach and the "Stop Nick Shirley Act" -- a California law restricting publication of personal information of immigration service providers -- and argues that while Nick's legalistic "fraud" label is sometimes too loose (a 23-year-old YouTuber with a fraud counter and no legal training), the underlying point is correct: a lot of state and federal grant money is going to programs that would shock the median taxpayer. [Nick Shirley and the "Stop Nick Shirley Act" are confirmed as real; the episode's framing of the union and legislative details are the host's claims.]
* **EU as the "other" side of manufactured discourse**: The hosts close on the EU, saying that European ruling classes -- described as "illegitimate" and "Macbeths" -- are moving from layered free-speech rollbacks to outright arrests for speech (UK, Germany) and a suspended Romanian election, and that the EU "cannot exist" alongside a free internet.
* **The white-pill closer**: After a long exchange about how much of American politics is a "fake marketplace of ideas" powered by political lubricant (every bill has some $50M line item for a donor-state program and $10M for a specific congressman), Brian says he is white-pilled: 3-5% GDP growth has held for 20 years *despite* the loss of the manufacturing base, and the software economy is "amazing," so the system is more robust than the doomers give it credit for. Lucas pushes back that white-pilling now only works if you first accept that things were broken for ~60 years and are only now being noticed.

The takeaway: Effort.New is a working proof that AI agents pointed at dense government databases can break real, document-backed stories that legacy media missed for years -- and that the same technique is now starting to shine on the "manufactured Internet" side, where privately funded NGOs, coordinated protests, and state-level grift make up a large share of the political and media landscape.
