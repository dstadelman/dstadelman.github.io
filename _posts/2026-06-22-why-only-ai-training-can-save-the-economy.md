## Summary

The American economy has become structurally dependent on AI infrastructure investment now, with AI spending driving roughly 39% of marginal GDP growth and expected to top $800 billion from Big Tech this year. But a structural crisis is emerging: enterprises are hitting AI budget caps while the labs need ever-increasing token consumption to justify that infrastructure spend. The article argues that broad-based AI training and education is the only mechanism that can align these opposing incentives -- moving workers from passive assisted usage to active agentic usage that justifies the cost.

* **AI investment is now the dominant economic force**: According to St. Louis Fed data, AI-related investment accounted for 39% of marginal GDP growth over the trailing four quarters -- outpacing the tech sector's 28% contribution at the height of the dot-com boom. Data centers, hardware, and networking alone hit 1.4% of US GDP in Q1 2026. Big Tech's AI capital expenditure is projected to exceed $800 billion in 2026, with White House AI czar David Sacks citing it as a potential 2.5-3% GDP tailwind.

* **The revenue model has shifted from seats to agents**: The old economics of $20-200/month per seat couldn't justify trillion-dollar infrastructure builds. The shift to agentic, usage-based consumption has been dramatic -- Anthropic's run-rate revenue jumped to $47 billion by late May 2026, driven not by new subscription users but by massive Claude Code token consumption. OpenAI's Q1 revenue hit $5.7 billion with its quad-core Codex coding tool. Enterprises spending at the $1M/year level jumped from 500 to over 1,000 in under two months.

* **Token scarcity is replacing token subsidies**: SemiAnalysis estimates that Anthropic's Claude Max plan at $200/month could cost the company up to $8,000 in tokens on the maximum tier, while ChatGPT Max could run up to $14,000. Companies like Uber and Walmart have already imposed $1,500/month caps per employee. GitHub Copilot moved to usage-based billing in April 2026, and Google introduced usage limits during I/O '26.

* **Enterprises are actively cutting token costs**: Model routing tools like Factory Router are becoming popular -- Matan Grinberg, CEO of Factory, reported $13 million saved in the first 30 days of private preview. Companies like Lindy have migrated from expensive US models to DeepSeek, which topped Ramp's trending SaaS vendors in June 2026. Cursor's Composer 2.5 achieves Opus 4.7 and GPT-5.5 benchmark levels at roughly one-tenth the cost.

* **The capability gap is the real bottleneck**: A KPMG and UT Austin study analyzing 1.4 million real workplace AI interactions found that only about 5% of users consistently demonstrate sophisticated AI collaboration behaviors -- treating AI as a reasoning partner, framing problems, iterating and pushing for deeper answers. An EY survey found just 28% of organizations have empowered employees to actually change business processes with AI. DataCamp noted that the most common training format -- video courses -- produces "awareness without confidence."

* **The labs are being forced toward enablement**: Both OpenAI and Anthropic have launched forward deployed engineering consulting efforts over the past six weeks. NLW predicts that token growth pressure will force the labs to dramatically increase investment in training and user enablement over the next 6-12 months -- if everyone caps at $1,500/month but the labs need everyone to spend at that cap, enablement becomes existential infrastructure.

* **The stakes are higher than most realize**: Budget caps create a "known ROI bias" -- people with spending limits gravitate toward incremental productivity gains rather than the unseemly experiments that create transformational value. The World Economic Forum has noted the half-life of skills has compressed from 30 years down to roughly 7 years, meaning AI education content decays faster than traditional training programs can ship. Managing agents is a new knowledge work primitive that every knowledge worker will need to master.

The fundamental argument is that AI training at mass scale is the only mechanism capable of bridging the gap between what enterprises need (cost control) and what the labs need (growth). Without broad enablement, token consumption plateaus and the entire AI infrastructure investment thesis collapses. With it, workers unlock use cases that make token costs seem negligible -- creating the virtuous cycle the economy now depends on.
