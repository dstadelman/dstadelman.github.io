[The AI Daily Brief - 10+ Things You Should Build With AI Instead of Sending Files](https://podcasters.spotify.com/pod/show/nlw/episodes/10-Things-You-Should-Build-With-AI-Instead-of-Sending-Files-e3keqlt)

OpenAI's Codex Sites announcement on June 2, 2026, which lets agents create and deploy hosted websites from natural language prompts, crystallized a wider shift NLW has been tracking: knowledge workers are increasingly publishing interactive links instead of static documents. The episode catalogs the underlying problems with traditional artifacts and offers 18 concrete examples of knowledge work outputs that work better as living websites. The argument is practical, not speculative -- the cost barrier to building deployable web artifacts has collapsed.

* **The Codex Sites catalyst:** OpenAI announced Sites at its "Intelligence at Work" event on June 2, 2026, bundling it with six role-specific plugins and expanded Annotations. Sites lets teams use natural language to build, deploy, and host interactive websites directly through the Codex app -- no separate hosting or infrastructure setup required. This represents a significant lowering of the barrier, especially for non-engineers.
* **11 problems with static documents, solved by websites:** The episode walks through a systematic list of failure modes -- version currency, distribution friction, navigation constraints, fragmented context across multiple files, lack of interactivity, flattened audience fit, low artifact reusability, no observability feedback, mediocre presentation, access control complexity, and agent-unfriendliness. Each one is illustrated with concrete knowledge work scenarios.
* **Cloudflare bot traffic milestone:** Cloudflare data shows AI agent and bot traffic hit 57.5% of all HTTP requests to HTML pages globally, surpassing human browsing for the first time -- months ahead of CEO Matthew Prince's 2027 forecast. This makes the podcast's point about designing knowledge artifacts for agent consumption increasingly urgent, not speculative.
* **18 concrete examples organized by pattern:** NLW groups the examples into three patterns -- things that get forwarded without you in the room, things that evolve after you "finish" them, and things that scatter across channels -- covering: narrative decks, strategy sites, research hubs, data sites, proposal microsites, client portals, project homepages, interactive case pages, competitive intelligence hubs, dynamic learning sites, living employee handbooks, board material portals, investor update pages, candidate sites, brand system sites, and press sites.
* **Enterprise AI sponsors discussed:** The episode mentions ZenoCoder (which launched Zenflow Work in April 2026 to orchestrate non-coding workflows across tools like JIRA, Gmail, and Notion), AssemblyAI's Voice Agent API ($4.50/hr flat-rate streaming voice AI), and OutSystems' Agentic Systems Platform announced at its ONE 2026 conference in Amsterdam. Robots & Pencils is also noted for its AWS-focused approach to production AI systems.

Websites are becoming the default knowledge work artifact not because they're trendy, but because the underlying economics of web deployment have fundamentally shifted. What once required hosting platforms, database backends, and engineering resources -- or at minimum, a specialist who understood deployment -- can now be done by anyone who can describe what they want in natural language. The 18 examples aren't meant to be prescriptive; the point is that the category of "what should be a website" is expanding faster than most people realize because the cost to build one has collapsed.
