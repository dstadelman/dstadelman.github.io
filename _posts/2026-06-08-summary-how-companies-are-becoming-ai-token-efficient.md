[The AI Daily Brief - How Companies Are Becoming AI Token Efficient](https://aidailybrief.beehiiv.com/p/how-companies-are-becoming-ai-token-efficient)

ChatGPT hits 1 billion monthly active users while companies wrestle with the operational reality of AI token costs. The conversation shifts from raw model capability to a more pragmatic question: how efficiently can AI get work done, and what does that mean for the industry?

* **ChatGPT reaches 1 billion MAU but at a 5-month delay**: Sensor Tower estimates confirm ChatGPT hit one billion monthly active app users in May 2026, taking only three and a half years -- the fastest any app has reached that milestone. However, OpenAI missed its end-of-2025 target, and the milestone arrived amid ongoing scrutiny of revenue targets and internal struggles. The code red declared in December 2025 and Fidji Simo's "end of side quests" memo in March did signal a resurgence, with GPT-5.5 (released April 23, 2026) and Codex driving renewed momentum. Claude's user base grew 640% year-over-year to 56 million MAU -- impressive growth that still puts it at roughly 5% of ChatGPT's consumer footprint. However, Anthropic is reportedly leading OpenAI in revenue, suggesting Claude's business-user base is disproportionately valuable. Most ChatGPT users who installed Claude also kept using ChatGPT at 95% of their prior rate, indicating Claude is being adopted as a secondary tool rather than a replacement.
* **Bots now outspeed humans on the web**: Cloudflare data shows bots represent 57.5% of HTTP requests to HTML pages globally -- overtaking human traffic for the first time in the internet's history, just months earlier than CEO Matthew Prince's 2027 prediction. Around 37% of bot traffic is now classified as "bad bots" that ignore robots.txt. The shift reflects the dual forces of AI data scraping and the rapid expansion of autonomous agents browsing and interacting with the web.
* **Meta launches Business Agent for small businesses**: At its Conversations 2026 conference in London, Meta unveiled Meta Business Agent -- an AI agent for WhatsApp, Messenger, and Instagram designed for small businesses rather than enterprise. One million businesses are already using the product in testing. Head of Product Naomi Gleit described it as an "enterprise play" but clarified the target is five-person companies, not large corporations. Agents handle appointment booking, order processing, and in the future market research and calendar management. The platform includes connectors for Shopify, Zendesk, and other non-Meta tools. Pricing will be free initially, shifting to subscription.
* **Token efficiency is now the core competitive metric**: Proplexity CEO Arav Srinivas argued on CNBC that the primary metric for the AI race will be "token value per watt per user" -- balancing accuracy, latency, cost, and privacy, not raw intelligence alone. This shift is reflected in benchmarks like Artificial Analysis's intelligence versus output tokens quadrant, which reveals that Claude Opus 4.7 and 4.8 achieve their top scores while consuming 80-90% more tokens than GPT-5.5, placing them outside the most efficient quadrant. Gemini 3.5 Flash's cost was more than 5x that of Gemini 3 Flash despite better performance. Perplexity demonstrated "hybrid-agentic inference" at Computex, automatically splitting tasks between local hardware (Intel Core Ultra 3) and cloud servers to balance cost, privacy, and intelligence.
* **Model routing and post-training emerge as first-class advantages**: Harvey and Fireworks AI published results showing hybrid agents -- GLM 5.1 routing to Claude Opus 4.7 only when needed (0.83 invocations per task) -- outperformed standalone Opus on both quality and cost ($368 vs $954). Post-training Kimi K2.6 on legal data pushed it ahead of Opus on a legal agent benchmark at 11x the cost advantage. Microsoft's frontier tuning program showed a McKinsey-deployed model delivering the highest win rate while being 10x cheaper than GPT-5.5 on specific tasks. Cursor's Composer 2.5 completes coding tasks at state-of-the-art quality with "radically higher efficiency" than both Claude and OpenAI equivalents. Factory Router, Perplexity's hybrid inference, and Glean CEO Arvind Jain's framework of four architectural levers (context quality, model routing, continual learning, harness design) all point to the same conclusion: the companies winning will be those best at routing each task to the right model at the right cost, not those using the most expensive model for everything.
* **The token economics story extends to enterprise adoption**: Ramp reported DeepSeek as its number one trending software vendor in June 2026, with US firms increasingly adopting the Chinese AI alternative for cost reasons. Uber capped Claude Code spending at $1,500/month, and Walmart began capping internal AI tool usage due to excessive consumption. The underlying dynamic is straightforward: the move from assisted AI to autonomous agents has created a supply-demand imbalance for compute, pushing labs away from subsidized per-seat pricing to usage-based API models -- which is why companies are imposing spending caps.

The central theme of the second half of 2026 is increasingly clear: it's not about which model is the smartest, but which one gets tasks done most efficiently. Token efficiency is no longer just an economics concern -- it has become the primary competitive differentiator across the entire stack, from model development to app-layer routing to enterprise AI architecture. The companies and teams that institutionalize smart routing, post-training, and cost-aware workflows will pull ahead of those still optimizing for raw capability alone.
