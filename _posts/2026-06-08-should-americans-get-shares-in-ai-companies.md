## Summary

The AI industry converged on a single week in late May and early June 2026: Nvidia unveiled the RTX Spark chip at GTC Taipei and announced Vera Rubin entered full production, Anthropic filed a confidential S-1, Alphabet announced a historic $80 billion equity raise, and Senator Bernie Sanders proposed a radical plan to give the American public a 50% stake in major AI companies. The convergence of these events turned AI ownership from a niche policy debate into a mainstream political question.

**Nvidia RTX Spark.** Nvidia unveiled the RTX Spark at Computex / GTC Taipei on May 31, 2026 -- a first-of-its-kind superchip combining 20 ARM CPU cores, 6,144 Blackwell GPU cores, 128GB of LPDDR5X unified memory, and 1 petaflop of FP4 AI compute. RTX Spark laptops and desktops will launch this fall from ASUS, Dell, HP, Lenovo, and Microsoft. Nvidia framed the chip as Windows's answer to Apple's M-series, designed to let PCs double as inference workhorses for local AI agents. Nvidia also announced Vera Rubin -- Vera (CPU) and Rubin (GPU) -- has entered full production, with OpenAI and Anthropic having taken delivery of first units.[The Verge][PCMag]

**Meta AI pendant plans.** Per The Information's May 29 report, Meta plans to start testing an AI pendant next year as part of a "Wearables for Work" hardware roadmap. The memo, authored by Alex Himel, VP of wearables at Meta, outlined three pillars: the pendant, expanded AI glasses, and an enterprise offering. The move follows Meta's acquisition of AI-wearables startup Limitless late last year and Reality Labs' continued hemorrhaging of $4 billion in operating losses last quarter.[The Information][Ars Technica]

**Meta AI support exploit.** Around May 31, hackers exploited Meta's AI support chatbot to hijack high-profile Instagram accounts including the Obama White House page and Sephora. Attackers simply asked the AI bot to link accounts to attacker-controlled email addresses during password resets. The exploit bypassed two-factor authentication entirely when combined with a VPN to spoof location data. The Guardian and 404 Media reported the incident.[404 Media][The Guardian]

**Anthropic IPO filing.** Anthropic confidentially submitted a draft S-1 to the SEC on June 1, 2026, with a Post-Series H valuation around $965 billion after a $65 billion funding round. Axios and Reuters confirmed the filing, which positions Anthropic ahead of OpenAI in the IPO queue.[Reuters]

**Alphabet $80 billion equity raise.** Alphabet (Google's parent) announced plans to raise $80 billion in equity offerings to fund AI infrastructure (including $190B in annual capex for 2026), with Berkshire Hathaway committing $10 billion. This marks the first new stock issuance in over two decades.[CNBC]

**Sanders' AI Sovereign Wealth Fund.** Senator Bernie Sanders published an op-ed in The New York Times (June 1) and announced the American AI Sovereign Wealth Fund Act, proposing a one-time 50% stock tax on OpenAI, Anthropic, and xAI to create a sovereign wealth fund that would provide dividend payments to citizens and fund health care, education, and housing. The NYTimes op-ed is titled "A.I. Is a Public Resource. You Should Own a Piece of It."[NYT]

The broader pattern across these stories is unmistakable: the financial scale of AI is accelerating (Alphabet's $80B raise, $965B Anthropic valuation, OpenAI's imminent IPO) while the policy debate shifts from whether AI should benefit the public to exactly how -- through taxation, nationalization, or public ownership stakes. Sanders' proposal is the hardest line, but even OpenAI's white papers have floated public wealth fund concepts, suggesting the direction of travel regardless of the specific policy vehicle.
