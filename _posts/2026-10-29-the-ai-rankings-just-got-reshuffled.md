# Summary: The AI Rankings Just Got Reshuffled

[The AI Daily Brief - The AI Rankings Just Got Reshuffled](https://podcasters.spotify.com/pod/show/nlw/episodes/The-AI-Rankings-Just-Got-Reshuffled-e3rc5gg)

Artificial Analysis overhauled the Intelligence Index everyone uses to rank frontier models, and version 5 reshuffles the leaderboard in a way most people say finally matches their lived experience: a clearer frontier tier, a much wider open-weights gap, and a new number one in Google's Gemini 4 Carbon, which landed just two weeks after Argon. The headlines edition also covers blowout cloud quarters from Microsoft and Google, a CapEx plateau that unfolded for reasons the bears didn't predict, and a DOJ investigation of Nvidia that turns out to be far broader than anyone thought.

* **Gemini 4 Carbon arrives two weeks after Argon**: one of the shortest gaps ever between versions of ultra-large models -- with a new base model in hand, Google can iterate quickly through reinforcement learning instead of waiting on a lengthy pre-training run, and Sundar Pichai said on Wednesday's earnings call that he expects "the next 12 months to see continued acceleration because of RSI."
* **The benchmarks are a definitive yes**: Carbon gained 8 points on Terminal-Bench 4.0 and 6 on Frontier Suite in coding, 12 on Exploit Gym and 3 on CWE Bench in cybersecurity, and 7 on OSWorld and 8 on Agent's Last Exam in computer use -- topping six of eight key benchmarks ahead of GPT-6 Astra and Opus 5.5.
* **Carbon is the first leader of the new AA Index V5**: it scored 59, a 9-point jump on the new scale, placing it ahead of Opus 5.5; on the AA Coding Index it landed second, tied with Sonnet 5.5 and three points behind Opus 5.5.
* **Cost sits mid-road for frontier models**: Artificial Analysis measured Carbon at $7.76 per task -- 23% cheaper than Opus 5.5 and 20% more expensive than GPT-6 Astra -- essentially unchanged from Argon.
* **The catch: you can't use it yet**: Google unveiled Carbon before general release, following the administration's pre-release model testing guidelines with a "deliberate approach" -- access starts this week with a subset of cyber defenders and trusted partners in its cyber defense program.
* **Arena's review underwhelms on renders, but not on feel**: Arena's Peter Gostev, who had testing access, published render and game-design tests that were generally slammed as less polished than Opus 5.5 -- though Gostev said Carbon feels a lot better than Argon, and NLW remains unconvinced 3D design is the right yardstick for LLMs.
* **There may be an even stronger Gemini waiting**: responding to an X commenter noting Fable 5.5 isn't here yet, a DeepMind researcher replied that an unreleased Gemini isn't here yet either -- prompting the observation that Google has another model better than Carbon, which has already hit number one on the AA Index.
* **Inside Google, launches now feel routine**: Gemini post-training researcher Xiao Zhang contrasted Carbon's quiet arrival with the big-bang celebration around Gemini 3 Pro -- weeks of dogfooding, overnight experiment loops, and swarms inspecting RL data have made number-one benchmark scores feel like "periodic checkpoints."
* **Builders are giving Google the benefit of the doubt**: despite roughly 10 months out of the early-adopter conversation, X commenters are reopening Google's Antigravity IDE and calling it "comeback season" -- though NLW's stance is to suspend judgment until people get hands on the model.
* **Microsoft broke out Azure for the first time -- and it's huge**: Azure grew 48% year-over-year to $33.2 billion, its first standalone line item, driving overall revenue up 19% to $92.3 billion, both slight beats; Microsoft also disclosed OpenAI contributed $10 billion of Azure revenue, roughly a third of the business, and the stock hit a new all-time high, up as much as 4.8% after hours.
* **Google Cloud doubled while Search stalls**: Google Cloud reached $30.7 billion, double the year-ago figure, nearly closing the gap with Microsoft -- but Search growth slowed to 12% from 17% and 19% the two prior quarters, YouTube posted a third consecutive slowdown to 9.2%, and after Pichai acknowledged AI Mode's lower monetization is weighing on results, the stock fell as much as 10% after hours.
* **The buildout may have hit peak velocity -- but not for the reason bears predicted**: Google, Microsoft, Oracle, and Meta all undershot this quarter's CapEx while holding forecasts steady (Google guiding a 50% increase to $300 billion next year); the bears expected faltering demand to prick the bubble, but hyperscalers appear to want to keep accelerating and are instead hitting physical constraints -- components, power, labor, and dwindling community support -- though analyst Luke Longo thought financing better explained the slowdown.
* **The DOJ's Nvidia probe is far bigger than anyone thought**: Reuters reports DOJ inquiries into whether Nvidia's $13 billion Hugging Face acquisition would let the chipmaker control a key distribution point for AI models -- and that these questions are part of a multi-year, top-to-bottom antitrust investigation that predates the Groq acqui-hire and also covers the traditional SchedMD and Run:ai acquisitions, venture investments, sales practices, and customer pressure, with no charges filed.
* **Index V5 goes majority-private to fight benchmark-maxing**: for the first time most benchmarks in the index run on private datasets so developers theoretically can't train to the test; V5 adds Cognition's Frontier Code 2.0 -- scored on whether a repo maintainer would merge the code -- and Terminal-Bench Science, upgrades Humanity's Last Exam to HLE Diamond plus saturation updates to AA-Omniscience and CritPt, swaps GDPval-AA for granular white-collar tests (AA-Briefcase, AutomationBench AA, and Surge's GDP.pdf), and drops SciCode and the long-context reasoning test AA-LCR.
* **The benchmark mix is a forward-looking bet**: the index is now equally weighted across four categories -- knowledge work, coding, science, and general -- with coding measured only agentically and science testing real agentic research rather than stored knowledge; the 25% science weighting reads as a bet on the science-and-math race Anthropic and OpenAI have been trading headlines over.
* **The reshuffle promoted agentic models and punished the rest**: before Carbon, Opus 5.5 led at 58; GPT-6 Astra jumped from sixth to second at 57, GPT-6.1 Sol rose from ninth to third, and GPT-6.1 Terra from tenth to fifth, while Sonnet 5.5 slipped to fourth, Meta's Muse Wonder Full fell from third to sixth, Fable 5.1 slid to eighth, and Kimi K3.1 Pro dropped all the way from fourth to ninth.
* **The open-weights gap blew out from 3 points to 12**: Kimi K3.1 Pro is the top open-weights model at 46, while DeepSeek V4.1 Flash and GLM-5.3 sit at 32 -- 26 points behind the leader and below Claude Haiku 5.5's 36; Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, and Meta all have models at 50+, followed by a 10-point cliff to the rest of the field.
* **Frontier costs range from 96 cents to $13 per task**: GPT-6.1 Terra is the cheapest top-10 model at $0.96 per task and Fable 5.1 the priciest at $13.04; among comparably sized models Opus and Sonnet run around $10, GPT-6 Astra $6.24, GPT-6.1 Sol just $1.27, and Gemini 4 Carbon, Grok 4.7, Muse Wonder, and Muse land in the high-$7 range.
* **The dominant reaction: the rankings finally match the vibes**: OpenAI Head of Strategic Futures Dean Ball, investor Haseeb Qureshi, and professor Ethan Mollick all said V5 tracks subjective experience far better and confirms both a deeper Anthropic/OpenAI lead and that no "Fable-class" open model has appeared yet.
* **The case for open weights: strong at agents, weak at science**: Brookings fellow Kyle Chan noted private benchmarks only rose from 45% to 60% of the index, and Kimi actually leads all models on Terminal-Bench 4.0 at 67% and beats Claude on the private AutomationBench AA -- its real deficit is agentic science (34% on Terminal-Bench Science vs. Astra's 63%), pointing to a genuine gap in advanced scientific reasoning rather than benchmark gaming.
* **The next fights are over weighting and effort settings**: does science matter as much as agentic coding for the average user -- or should we skip the headline number and watch category benchmarks instead; and since leaderboards run on max settings nobody actually uses, NLW thinks the index should be sortable by effort as well as by model.
* **A new Progress Index charts every model since November 2022**: the long-term measure tracks how each lab's leading model improves over time, and people quickly mined it -- one analysis concluded OpenAI has the best reinforcement learning "or their models just suck without reasoning," while another estimated the US-China model gap at 3.3 months and closing.

Benchmarks are inherently limited but indispensable, and the Intelligence Index has always been a cat-and-mouse game against reality -- V5's private datasets, agentic emphasis, and dropped long-context tests are the latest move, and the reshuffle it produced finally matches how people actually experience these models. The bigger story is structural: a clear frontier tier of four US labs, a widening gulf with open weights, and Google suddenly back on top -- with the caveat that the number-one model still can't be used by anyone outside Google's cyber defense program.
