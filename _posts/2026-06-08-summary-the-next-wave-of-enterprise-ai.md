[The AI Daily Brief - The Next Wave of Enterprise AI](https://d3ctxlq1ktw2nl.cloudfront.net/staging/2026-5-3/425460343-44100-2-2d98e8ffc691d.mp3)

Nathaniel Whittemore's AI Daily Brief covers the shifting landscape of enterprise AI adoption against a backdrop of federal regulatory uncertainty, infrastructure constraints, and massive product launches from OpenAI and Microsoft. The core thesis: organizations are moving from the "subsidy era" of AI into a "scarcity era" where cost management and operational fluency matter as much as raw capability.

* **Trump's AI Executive Order**: The episode opens with the latest Trump administration executive order on AI model safety testing. The order replaces an initial proposal requiring 90-day advance model sharing with a 30-day window. David Sacks (AI Policy Czar) intervened at the last hour to pull the more restrictive draft. The order makes safety testing voluntary for labs, establishes an NSA-led testing framework, and creates a cybersecurity clearinghouse run by Treasury in consultation with NSA, DHS, and CISA. It does not give the government authority to block model releases. The order was signed quietly with no ceremony -- suggesting discomfort with the topic. Critics from both right (Steve Bannon) and left (Bernie Sanders) agree it does too little. [unverified]
* **Anthropic's Mythos/Project Glasswing**: Anthropic expanded access to its **mythos** model through the Project Glasswing safety program to 150 new partners across 15 countries spanning energy, water, communications, healthcare, and computer hardware. The episode notes Anthropic's messaging on general availability has softened from "in the coming weeks" (said during Opus 4.8 rollout) to acknowledging the need for safeguards that "we, and to our knowledge, all other AI developers have yet to develop." Testers report the model is powerful but "eye-wateringly expensive," with Anthropic subsidizing early use. [unverified]
* **OpenAI Codex Knowledge Work Report**: Microsoft Build event opened with OpenAI's report positioning **Codex** as the interface for the next generation of knowledge work, reaching 5 million weekly active users with non-technical knowledge workers growing 3x faster than developer adoption. The report identifies three core frictions in knowledge work: finding relevant inputs across siloed systems, information coordination across tools, and approval/verification workflows. Key product highlights: **Annotations** (pointing to specific document sections rather than describing them), **role-specific plugins** for sales, data analytics, creative production, product design, equity investing, and investment banking, and **Sites** (turning artifacts into shareable web apps/URLs). The episode author argues this makes "building websites and disposable web apps" a new core knowledge work primitive. [unverified]
* **Microsoft Build Model Family**: Microsoft unveiled seven new in-house models at Build, headlined by **ma-1 thinking**, a 1.3 trillion parameter mixture-of-experts model trained without synthetic data or distillation from prior models. Microsoft's strategic positioning treats these not as benchmark competitors but as inputs to "Microsoft Frontier Tuning" -- customizing models for specific enterprise use cases at lower cost. Early adopter McKinsey reportedly showed outputs that outperformed GPT-5.5 on quality at 10x lower cost. Satya Nadella described this as a "pretty significant shift" in Microsoft's enterprise AI approach. [unverified]
* **Uber Token Spending Cap**: Uber implemented a $1,500 monthly per-employee spending cap on AI token usage across the organization. The episode flags this as evidence that cost management is becoming a central organizing vector for enterprise AI strategy, not just technical capability. [unverified]
* **SK Hynix HBM Supply Expansion**: SK Hynix announced plans to **double** its high-bandwidth memory (HBM) manufacturing capacity by the end of the decade, acknowledging that AI-driven demand represents a structural shift. SK Hynix chairman **Shinhwa Jang** noted the HBM shortage could persist through 2030, but new factory construction takes 3-4 years, meaning supply won't fully catch up to demand in the near term. [unverified]
* **KPMG/UT Austin Research**: Sponsored segment citing KPMG and University of Texas at Austin research analyzing 1.4 million real workplace AI interactions. Key finding: highest-impact AI users treat AI as a "reasoning partner" -- framing problems, guiding thinking, iterating, and pushing for better answers -- rather than as a prompt-response tool. These behaviors are teachable at scale. [unverified]

Both OpenAI and Microsoft used recent product launches to address the core challenge of enterprise AI adoption at scale: not just making models better, but making AI cheaper, more accessible, and more integrated into existing workflows. The race for the next wave of enterprise adoption is fully underway, and the winners will be companies that solve the economics of scaling, not just the engineering of capability.
