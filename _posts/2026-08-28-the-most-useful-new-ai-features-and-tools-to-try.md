# Summary: The Most Useful New AI Features and Tools to Try

[The AI Daily Brief - The Most Useful New AI Features and Tools to Try](https://podcasters.spotify.com/pod/show/nlw/episodes/The-Most-Useful-New-AI-Features-and-Tools-to-Try-e3o1h3t)

This week had no single marquee model drop; instead a dozen shipped features plus a handful of headline business stories quietly redrew how AI actually gets used in everyday work. Claude gained its own browser, ChatGPT Work learned to slide fluidly between temporary and permanent mode, Google's new transcription model started capturing your intent rather than just your words, and AI video crossed the line where generating a clip takes less time than it takes to watch it. Laid against that backdrop, the big money stories landed: NVIDIA confirmed it is buying Hugging Face for $12.9 billion and nearly posted a $100 billion quarter, while Salesforce posted the SaaS comeback of the season.

* **NVIDIA confirmed the Hugging Face buy for $12.9B**: The Information broke the story late Wednesday after NVIDIA's earnings call; Hugging Face has roughly $150M in annualized revenue, so NVIDIA is paying around 80x revenue (versus ~15x for SpaceX's $60B Cursor acquisition). NVIDIA is buying the premier distribution channel for open models, not the revenue.
* **The move is offense and defense at once**: the $6B Poolside licensing deal that pulled in about 100 veteran AI researchers, a strategic Perplexity partnership for harnesses and app-layer play, and Nemotron (NVIDIA's open-model lineup) going from tech demo to real contender all point one direction -- a full-stack open business. Defense, too: as NVIDIA's biggest customers build custom silicon, open models that are downloaded, fine-tuned, and served on NVIDIA GPUs through CUDA are the counterweight.
* **The neutrality question lingers, but trust held**: Hugging Face's value leans partly on its perceived independence -- Eric Hartford calls the exit a loss for open source and "an opportunity for a new standard bearer to arise." The reaction nonetheless showed unusually more optimism than a normal acquisition, a tell of how much trust NVIDIA has built with the AI community.
* **NVIDIA's blowout quarter came with a yellow flag**: $96.2B in quarterly sales and 106% year-over-year growth, tantalizingly close to a $100B quarter, but with free cash flow down 50% to $21B, days sales outstanding at 60 (up from 45), and "investment-grade customers" granted longer payment terms. CFO Colette Kress guided to 89.5% for Q3 and 70% for fiscal 2027 on supply-chain constraints, and Amazon ordered 2 million extra chips (stock up 4.8% after-hours).
* **NVIDIA paused its neocloud revenue-sharing program**: per the Wall Street Journal, the two-month-old plan to give customers credit support in exchange for a cut of profits is on hold after employees flagged antitrust and control concerns; NVIDIA denies the reporting and says the model "is still in place and continues to evolve."
* **Salesforce led the SaaS comeback, up 22% in a day**: Agentforce is on track for $1.5B in revenue this year (up from a $1.2B Q1 forecast), quarterly sales grew 11% to $11.5B, and Salesforce is now within 5% of erasing the entire SaaSpocalypse draw-down. Benioff touted near-record-low attrition, more-than-doubled bookings, and sixfold agentic activity -- with former White House AI czar David Sacks declaring the "AI capex is a bubble / SaaS is dead" narratives "getting shredded."
* **The app layer is tripling across the board**: per The Information, Cognition (Devin) hit $900M annualized revenue (3x since January), Perplexity tripled to $750M, Higgsfield tripled to $700M, OpenEvidence doubled to $300M, and Manus -- even after the messy Meta unwind -- claims $400M. DeepSeek's $70M this year is a ~10x jump on its full 2025 total.
* **A FINRA-for-AI executive order stalled out**: a drafted EO would have created an industry self-regulatory body modeled on FINRA -- the idea DeepMind's Demis Hassabis proposed in a July post and Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent publicly backed -- and reportedly ran into David Sacks, who called it "a horrible idea and a Trojan horse for an open source model ban" on the All In podcast.
* **100+ companies declared a cyber-defense emergency**: an open letter signed by OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Microsoft, AWS, plus Visa, General Motors, and PwC warned that AI-enabled cyberattacks will become "far more widespread and sophisticated" within months, urging shared AI tooling and cross-industry coordination before a closing "defender's window" -- with Sam Altman publicly positioning security above competition.
* **Claude got its own browser**: a window now opens alongside a session for Claude to use when a task touches a website -- filling forms, working a web app, agentic browsing -- built directly into the desktop app and kept separate from your own browser and logins (Claude-in-Chrome still there for those who want it); a16z's Martin Casado calls it the step from "fancy RPA" to a virtual coworker.
* **ChatGPT Work gained a cloud computer and a privacy upgrade**: OpenAI pitches life tasks (book a DMV or passport appointment, set up utilities, compare insurance), and its head of developer experience Romain Huet says the possibilities "start to feel almost limitless." Temporary chats can now be personalized with your existing memories, custom instructions, and plugins, and upgraded to saved history when worth keeping.
* **A long-wanted connector fix landed**: plugins can now connect multiple Gmail accounts at once -- a permanent power-user wishlist item, and the same unconstrained-connector flexibility a commenter named Claire Vo praised in the new Grok.
* **Open source kept pace as the big labs shipped**: the Hermes agent added real-profile browsing (acting with your own Chrome logins from a managed copy of the profile), Bot Mode (named specialist bots with their own roles, models, memories, and skills that can talk to each other), and a HUD overlay mode -- open source, any provider or model, and a fraction of the cost of a two-hundred-dollar enterprise agent.
* **Salesforce and Anthropic launched Claude Force**: a new set of plugins and skills lets Claude agents work the CRM directly -- Salesforce data as context, agentic actions respecting existing governance guardrails via the Agentforce harness. Benioff: "Fusing Claude's extraordinary reasoning with the trusted data, workflows, and governance every enterprise runs on -- this is how every business will run." It even got a Matthew McConaughey ad.
* **The Claude Force signal matters more than the jokes**: Thomas Fanning says labs routing through software vendors instead of around them acknowledges that data, embedded workflows, and incumbency remain software's moats; the episode says enterprise people it spoke to were extremely excited, with one shelving plans to build a Salesforce alternative to see whether this simply does the job.
* **Google shipped a cleanup-first voice model and a control-first video model**: Gemini 3.5 Transcribe supports 85+ languages plus a "smart translate" mode that strips filler and self-corrections to capture intent (NLW notes that good cleanup is the genuinely hard part, judging by endlessly toggling WhisperFlow settings). Gemini Omni 1.1 Flash adds frame-level start/end control, video input references, 4K upscaling, and 360p fast previews, landing #1 in the text-to-video arena.
* **Voice and video crossed speed lines**: Grok Voice Think (SpaceXAI) now handles 15,000 Starlink customer support and sales calls a day, ships replacements, and fulfills 3,000+ orders a week while topping the Artificial Analysis speech-to-speech index; Fal's H3 Max produces video in an average 3.49 seconds -- faster than it takes to watch -- with one tester generating a clip in 1.3 seconds, trialing on Venice, which just crossed $100M ARR.

The primary point of the episode is that the line between an AI that gives answers and an AI that operates on your behalf just got a lot thinner -- Claude's browser, ChatGPT Work's cloud computer, and Grok all landed within weeks, which is where the assistant-to-operator shift really bites. Layered on the headlines, the through-line is that both frontier labs and incumbents are converging on the same enterprise reality: embedded data, governance, and workflow (as in NVIDIA's Hugging Face acquisition and Salesforce's Agentforce-driven SaaS renaissance) are now the real moat, and the features people will actually use this week are the practical ones.
