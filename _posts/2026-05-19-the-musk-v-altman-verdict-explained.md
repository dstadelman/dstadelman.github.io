A federal jury in Oakland unanimously rejected all of Elon Musk's claims against OpenAI and CEO Sam Altman on May 18, 2026 [1]. They deliberated less than two hours. The reason was straightforward: the jury found Musk had waited too long to file [2].

Musk filed the lawsuit in 2024, accusing Altman, co-founder Greg Brockman, and OpenAI itself of abandoning the organization's founding commitment to operate as a nonprofit for the benefit of humanity [3]. The case was filed in the Northern District of California. Musk sought to oust Altman and force OpenAI back into nonprofit status [2]. He valued his claim at $134 billion, though the New York Times reported the suit at $150 billion [4,5].

## What Musk Alleged

The core of Musk's case rested on a timeline that stretched back over a decade. He claimed that between 2016 and 2020, he had donated $38 million to OpenAI based on the understanding that it would remain a nonprofit [3]. His argument was that this money had been misused once the company shifted to a for-profit capped-profit structure.

Musk also alleged that Microsoft had aided and abetted this breach of trust in its role as OpenAI's primary commercial partner [6]. The claim for breach of charitable trust was specifically barred by the statute of limitations, meaning the court found the period during which such a claim could be brought had expired [6].

## How the Trial Unfolded

The trial began in late April 2026 and ran for three weeks [7]. On day four, Musk took the stand himself for cross-examination, where opposing lawyers pressed him on his timeline and motives [8]. The trial was widely framed in Silicon Valley as a clash between two of its most prominent figures [9].

During the proceedings, OpenAI's position was that Musk was driven by jealousy and regret for having walked away from the company years earlier [4]. The defense emphasized that Altman had openly and publicly announced OpenAI's shift to a for-profit model long before Musk filed his suit, making the statute of limitations a central factual dispute.

## The Verdict

The jury returned a unanimous verdict in OpenAI's favor after less than two hours of deliberation [2]. Their findings addressed several distinct claims:

- The breach of charitable trust claim was barred by the statute of limitations [6].
- The claim that Microsoft aided and abetted the breach was also dismissed on the same grounds [6].
- The jury agreed that Musk had waited too long to file his lawsuit, leaving all of his claims essentially expired [10].

As the Guardian reported, the jury found that Musk's 2024 filing did not fall within the statute of limitations for bringing his case [10]. The New York Times framed the outcome as a high legal bar that Musk's case could not clear [4].

## What Musk Said After

Musk immediately dismissed the verdict. He called it a "technicality" and pledged to appeal [1]. The statutory clock was the dispositive issue -- the jury did not rule on the truth or falsity of Musk's underlying claims about how OpenAI was run, only on whether he filed within the legally permitted window. This distinction matters because it leaves the factual questions about OpenAI's governance unresolved.

## Why This Matters

The verdict keeps OpenAI on its current trajectory as a for-profit entity under Sam Altman's leadership. The company can continue its commercial relationships and development trajectory without the threat of a court order forcing a return to nonprofit status [4].

For the broader AI landscape, the outcome means the rivalry between Musk's xAI (which operates Grok) and OpenAI (which powers ChatGPT) will continue as a competitive and commercial contest rather than a legal one. Both are now entrenched as commercial enterprises, and this verdict has effectively removed the remaining legal avenue through which Musk could have challenged OpenAI's corporate structure.

The statute of limitations issue also sends a message to Silicon Valley founders and early investors: commitment disputes have deadlines. Allegations about how a company's mission was altered at its founding are not forever actionable, even if the alleged betrayal feels current.
