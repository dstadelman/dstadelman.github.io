# Summary: The AI vs AI Battle for the Internet

[The AI Daily Brief - The AI vs AI Battle for the Internet](https://podcasters.spotify.com/pod/show/nlw/episodes/The-AI-vs-AI-Battle-for-the-Internet-e3qu3ad)

Anthropic launched Claude Duet, its proactive personal agent -- and it still could not hold the main slot of this episode. The PHP2Shell vulnerability touching perhaps 70% of the web, a breach at Railway, and millions of AI-driven exploit attempts within hours of disclosure mark what may be the transition between two eras of cybersecurity: one where long-held norms made exploits manageable, and a new one where it is AI versus AI and the winner is whoever coordinates fastest.

* **Claude Duet**: Anthropic's answer to Grok Bot, Meta's Muse and OpenAI's Dots, announced October 21 and aimed at professionals and small businesses -- a morning brief across connected apps, alerts only when something needs attention, and tasks from scheduling to dashboards, forecasts and prototyping web apps, with a virtual machine, persistent memory, thousands of connectors, and safety guardrails like explicit approval before payments or deletions.
* **The Opus 5.5 differentiator**: for much of the community, summed up by Dan McAteer, the big immediate differentiator was not the proactivity but that Duet is built on Opus 5.5; NLW bets this is part one of a pre-IPO one-two punch -- release the agent, then ship a new model (perhaps Fable 5.5) that makes everyone feel far behind.
* **The personal agent race**: pretty much every major AI company now has a personal agent except Google -- Meta's Muse, Anthropic's Duet, OpenAI's Dots, SpaceX's Bots -- and the competition is shifting from who has the smartest model to who owns the persistent relationship with the user, arguably because peak intelligence for most workflows was reached late last year or earlier this year.
* **Walmart welcomes third-party agents**: while Amazon, JPMorgan and Bank of America say no, Walmart is launching Agent Profiles and a Walmart Agent Gateway next month with configurable spending limits, approval by default, no agent changes to payment methods, and returns protection even when a compliant agent errs -- its own Sparky agent drives order values 40% higher than non-agentic shoppers.
* **Agentic shopping is real**: Meta said on its Q3 earnings call that 25% of active Muse users already shop with the agent, at an average purchase value of $200 -- numbers that surprised even e-commerce veterans and make Walmart's welcome mat look strategic.
* **Meta funds the buildout with non-voting stock**: alongside Q3 earnings, Meta announced a new class of non-voting Class C stock trading from January and raised the low end of this year's data-center capex to $135-145 billion from $130 billion; Moelis told the board Meta could raise roughly $700 billion without Zuckerberg's vote falling below 50%, and the stock was up in after-hours trading despite dilution concerns.
* **Project Glasswing warned this was coming**: Anthropic's April 2026 announcement gave a small set of government and corporate actors defensive access to its Mythos model after its preview found thousands of high-severity vulnerabilities in every major operating system and web browser -- with a consumer version of that capability later arriving as Fable.
* **PHP2Shell is the defender's worst nightmare**: disclosed October 21 and potentially affecting as much as 70% of websites and web apps, the flaw allows unauthenticated remote code execution on any PHP backend -- even the latest versions -- and was discovered around two months earlier using GPT-5.6 Sol by a security researcher at Calif and independently by the team at Wiz.
* **Worse than any PHP flaw in a decade**: Volker Dusch, a security engineer at the nonprofit that maintains PHP, called it the most severe security issue the language has faced in over a decade, comparing it to 2014's Heartbleed.
* **The defender's window closed 10 hours early**: knowledge of the exploit spread roughly ten hours ahead of disclosure, before anyone could patch; the NSA recommended organizations take systems offline until patched and the FBI's Cyber Division asked organizations to report intrusions.
* **Why they could not just ship the patch first**: the patch itself makes the exploit obvious, so releasing it is effectively releasing the vulnerability to a storm of script kiddies scanning everything they find -- which is why the NSA told everyone to get ready to update the moment the patch landed.
* **Attack volume scaled at machine speed**: Cloudflare CEO Matthew Prince reported thousands of exploit attempts in the ten hours before the announcement, tens of thousands within 45 minutes, hundreds of thousands within two hours, and millions now.
* **A legacy of lost media**: 4chan went read-only, Something Awful shut its forums, and Pinboard, Choice of Games, Damn Interesting and the Explain xkcd wiki went offline -- with lament that 15 years of old PHP sites may simply never come back.
* **Railway gets hit too**: the AI hosting platform (similar to Vercel) disclosed that attackers accessed encrypted customer environment variables and decrypted some -- API keys and credentials -- with later forensics putting the blast radius at about 1.85% of workspaces and suggesting the attacker was hunting AI provider keys for model distillation, mirroring the OpenCode breach that hit about 1.4% of Console users.
* **The new normal, per OpenCode's Dax Raad**: telling the Railway team that attackers will not stop, will get more sophisticated, will target every surface and every employee, and will find long-forgotten projects to use against you -- "the only reason we're surviving is because we have agents going 24-7."
* **Fleets versus swarms**: former OpenAI staffer Joshua Achiam compared the moment to March 1st, 2020 for cyber with possible internet lockdowns; entrepreneur John Ennis countered that you cannot stop a nuclear weapon with another nuclear weapon but you can stop an AI attack with an AI; Box's Aaron Levie predicts we will burn 100x more tokens on the attack-defense loop, scanning every log and every line of code, with more talent needed to point agents and respond to incidents; Embroidery co-founder Zack Korman says the "ban AI models" crowd should instead fix their own infrastructure; and Enclave CEO Tal Hoffman frames the future as governed agent fleets on defense versus cheap, relentless swarms on offense -- whoever coordinates faster wins.
* **New approaches needed**: OpenAI President Greg Brockman, in "Towards securing the internet," floated authorized defenders such as government scanning the internet for vulnerable hosts and, in extreme cases, patching those systems remotely; a16z's Martin Casado offers the most optimistic take -- AI will make software far more capable, secure and reliable, "but before that, things are going to get much, much worse."

Cybersecurity has entered a new regime: frontier models find internet-scale vulnerabilities before attackers do, but leaks, patch-as-exploit dynamics and agent swarms compress the battle to machine speed, where the decisive advantage is coordination rather than raw intelligence. NLW's stance is long-term optimism without illusion -- the transition itself carries real chaos, pain and agony, and for cybersecurity, we are already in it.
