# Summary | The New Enterprise Battle Over Who Owns the Model

[AI Daily Brief - The New Enterprise Battle Over Who Owns the Model](https://podcasters.spotify.com/pod/show/nlw/episodes/The-New-Enterprise-Battle-Over-Who-Owns-the-Model-e3m65k0)

In this episode, AI Daily Brief examines the growing enterprise battle over who controls foundational AI models -- from Microsoft's internal ma-model push to Thinking Machines Lab's open-weight Inkling. The conversation spans Cursor Labs' post-SpaceX strategy, Anthropic's upcoming IPO window, NVIDIA's robotics-focused Cosmos 3 Edge, and Apple's search for AI server-chip acquisitions.

* **Thinking Machines Lab releases open-weight Inkling**: Mira Muradi's TML (former OpenAI CTO) released its first foundation model called Inkling, a 975-billion-parameter mixture-of-experts architecture with 41 billion active parameters. It supports a million-token context window and processes text, images, and audio natively -- along with the preview of a smaller "Inkling Small" at 12 billion parameters built on TML's fine-tuning platform called Tinker. Crucially, Inkling was pre-trained largely from scratch; unlike many Chinese peers whose training data distills from OpenAI or Anthropic outputs, TML used its own human-created and synthetic data -- making it potentially the first major open frontier model not derived from those two closed models. Benchmarks place it competitive but not state-of-the-art on reasoning and coding tasks, and initial community reception was mixed to negative on quality while praising the training lineage.

* **Microsoft pushes ma-models as a sales weapon**: Bloomberg reported Microsoft is preparing its sales teams to compete directly against OpenAI and Anthropic using its own ma-prefix models (lowercase) in Copilot, emphasizing cost efficiency and data sovereignty advantages over Claude and GPT models. The strategy includes framing competitors as "slower and less accurate" within the Office ecosystem and positioning customers toward Microsoft's vertically integrated stack. CEO Satya Nadella has publicly warned enterprises about trusting external model providers with sensitive data given their competitive incentives -- aligning with a new product called Microsoft Frontier Tuning that lets companies fine-tune ma-models on internal data while still relying on Microsoft's infrastructure.

* **SpaceX stock dips below IPO price**: For the first time since its public listing, SpaceX shares fell below the $135 IPO price, trading as low as $133 before recovering close to $135.27 -- marking a 33% decline from all-time highs and Elon Musk losing his trillionaire status. More broadly, the dip raises questions about other AI companies' IPO timing: OpenAI advisors reportedly told Sam Altman they doubt a trillion-dollar valuation is achievable in current conditions, while Anthropic appears to be pressing ahead with investment bank meetings and a credit facility targetting an IPO as early as September or October -- testifying their confidence.

* **Cursor Labs post-SpaceX pivot toward model development**: In a May internal meeting (transcript name corrupted via audio), Cursor's CEO laid out the company's intention to become a top-tier independent model developer, not merely an AI coding tool subsidiary under SpaceX ownership. The plan targets a state-of-the-art model by year-end with significant compute advantage accumulation by 2027. Behind the scenes, Cursor reportedly operates on plans to expand beyond coding -- potentially building a Claude-equivalent competitor -- while the first product of the Cursor-SPacex partnership is Grok 4.5 (previously referred to as "Grock," confirmed via industry naming conventions).

* **NVIDIA enters robotics with Cosmos 3 Edge**: NVIDIA released Cosmos 3 Edge, a small physical-AI model at just 4 billion parameters designed for edge devices in robots. It functions dual-purpose as both world model and vision-language model standard LLM architecture simultaneously. This follows NVIDIA's launch late last month of Nemotron 3 Ultra, their larger open-weight model addressing US-based compute demand amid spiraling token costs. NVIDIA also expanded partnerships including a new robotics-focused agreement with Toyota.

* **Apple shopping for AI chip acquisitions**: Apple is reportedly in discussions with investment bankers and semiconductor startups to acquire an AI server-chip company -- a notable departure from its history of shunning large deals (the Beats acquisition at $3 billion remains the largest). With delayed development of its own server-grade Baltra processor, Apple currently relies on Google to develop PowerSiri models and runs server infrastructure on NVIDIA chips via Google Cloud rather than using M-series chips for enterprise inference -- a gap AI-era demands may be forcing action.

* **Enterprises tuning their own models is emerging**: The convergence of Microsoft's ma-model push, Tinker by Thinking Machines Lab, and broader enterprise anxiety about data sovereignty suggests fine-tuning customized open weights models on private infrastructure is an emerging strategic wedge. However, critics like Simon Smith argue this bet runs counter to the "bitter lesson" in ML: that more data and compute generally beat carefully curated human expertise, and the ongoing costs of maintaining fine-tuned pipelines (data collection, curation, training, deployment) often exceed the value of a general-purpose frontier model with context.

What makes this episode notable is not any single company's move but the structural trend: enterprises are no longer simply choosing between OpenAI or Anthropic -- they're evaluating open-weight stacks, proprietary fine-tuning services, and hybrid approaches where companies retain sovereignty over their learning pipelines. The next six to twelve months of experimentation will determine which models and architectures endure and which fade into early adopter graveyard alongside failed vertical AI startups in the process.
