# Summary: The AI Token Shortage Begins [AI Monthly Recap]

[AI Daily Brief - The AI Token Shortage Begins [AI Monthly Recap]](https://podcasters.spotify.com/pod/show/nlw/episodes/The-AI-Token-Shortage-Begins-AI-Monthly-Recap-e3k6e0o)

This episode of the AI Daily Brief covers May 2026 as one of the most consequential months in AI history, marking the transition from the AI subsidy era into an era of token scarcity. It traces the economic and technological shifts that reshaped the industry, from Anthropic's staggering growth to new business models and the beginning of a structural token shortage.

* **Anthropic's $65 billion round**: Anthropic closed a $65 billion fundraising round at a $965 billion valuation, surpassing OpenAI in business adoption per Ramp data. The company was on track for its first profitable quarter -- potentially the first of any foundation model lab -- with annualized revenue approaching $47 billion, up from $3 billion at the start of 2025.
* **The end of the AI subsidy era**: Companies shifted from flat-seat pricing to usage-based billing as the economic reality of token consumption became clear. GitHub Copilot eliminated its premium request model at month's end, Google I/O introduced usage limits on Gemini Ultra plans, and Anthropic moved to per-token billing for third-party tool users, creating significant cost shock for power users.
* **Uber burned its 2026 AI budget in four months**: After the CTO revealed Uber had spent its entire annual AI budget in just four months, the story cascaded into broader "AI sticker shock" conversations. An internal leaderboard called KiroRank was scrapped after Amazon employees gamed it by generating unnecessary AI tasks to boost their scores.
* **Market-based responses to token scarcity**: Cursor released Composer 2.5 (built on Moonshot's Kimi K2.5 checkpoint) at lower cost than Opus 4.5 or GPT-5.5. Google pushed Gemini 3.0 Flash as a cost-saving option, though actual usage showed Gemini 3.5 Flash costs roughly 5x more. DeepSeek made its 75% price cut on V4-Pro permanent on May 23, targeting cost-conscious enterprises.
* **SpaceX becomes a cloud provider**: On May 6, SpaceX announced a compute partnership allowing Anthropic access to Colossus 1, with follow-up deals extending to Colossus 2 through May 2029 at $1.25 billion per month. This repositioned Elon Musk from AI antagonist to infrastructure kingmaker, with major implications for the upcoming SpaceX IPO.
* **Inference infrastructure surging**: Base10 raised $1 billion at an $11 billion valuation, OpenRouter raised $113 million Series B, and AI memory stocks surged with SK Hynix and Micron hitting trillion-dollar valuations. Meta began positioning itself as a cloud infrastructure player, seeking to de-risk its $130 billion compute investments.
* **Opus 4.8 and the shift from models to harnesses**: Claude Opus 4.8 shipped May 28, but the real innovation was in tooling -- Slash Goal (launched in Claude Code 2.1.139 on May 12) and dynamic workflows, not model leaps. The creator community increasingly prioritized better developer surfaces over incremental model gains.
* **OpenAI's enterprise push**: OpenAI launched the OpenAI Deployment Company on May 11, a $4 billion venture to embed Forward Deployed Engineers inside client organizations -- paralleling Anthropic's partner approach with Blackstone, Hellman & Friedman, and Goldman Sachs.
* **Narrative shifts from leadership**: Sam Altman and Dario Amodei both pulled back from the catastrophic job-displacement narrative they had previously championed, opening space for more nuanced policy discussion. Meanwhile, the White House opposed Anthropic's Mythos expansion partly over token shortage concerns -- essentially reserving compute capacity for government use.
* **The path forward**: The episode looks ahead to June, highlighting the SpaceX IPO as a critical signal for how the market prices AI infrastructure, plus expected releases of another OpenAI model and a version of Anthropic's Mythos. The central challenge: enterprises that adapt to token-scarcity economics fastest will gain serious competitive advantage.

The overarching story of May 2026 is the collision between unprecedented AI demand and finite computational supply. The AI subsidy era -- where power users got thousands of dollars of token value for a $200-a-month subscription -- is ending. What's replacing it is a structural token shortage driving massive shifts: usage-based billing everywhere, new infrastructure layer consolidation, government involvement in compute allocation, and a race to build cheaper AI without sacrificing intelligence. Companies are caught between the old paradigm of unlimited AI experimentation and a new reality where every token has real monetary cost. The winners will be those who manage this transition most efficiently.
