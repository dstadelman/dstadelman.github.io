# Summary: Grok 4.6 Shows How Fast Your AI Options Are Expanding

[AI Daily Brief - Grok 4.6 Shows How Fast Your AI Options Are Expanding](https://podcasters.spotify.com/pod/show/nlw/episodes/Grok-4-6-Shows-How-Fast-Your-AI-Options-Are-Expanding-e3ncdop)

The episode covers the release of Grok 4.6 from SpaceX AI (xAI), whose benchmarks put the company squarely back in the frontier model race after months of being written off, alongside a wave of big-money news across the AI industry. Coverage spans Cognition seeking a $40 billion round just three months after pricing at $26 billion, Lovable's $400 million Series C, the neocloud earnings from CoreWeave and Nebius, Tencent tripling its AI capex, leaked DeepSeek V4 Pro benchmarks, and the US administration's pivot to include open models in its safety-testing framework.

* **Grok 4.6 benchmarks put xAI back at the frontier table**: SpaceX AI reported that Grok 4.6 matches GPT-5.6 Sol on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index (both at 61, up five points from Grok 4.5's 56), edges ahead of Kimi K3, and lands a point or two behind Fable 5 and Claude Opus 5 [x.ai/news/grok-4-6](https://x.ai/news/grok-4-6) [VentureBeat - August 13, 2026](https://venturebeat.com/technology/spacexai-debuts-grok-4-6-overtaking-kimi-k3s-performance-and-matching-gpt-5-6-sol-for-worlds-third-best-on-artificial-analysis). The company claims Grok 4.6 overtakes both GPT-5.6 Sol and Fable 5 on GDPVal, the measure of agentic performance on economically valuable tasks, and sits between the two on Cursor Bench, though a few points behind on DeepSWE and Terminal Bench [Artificial Analysis](https://artificialanalysis.ai/models/grok-4-6) [MindStudio - August 2026](https://www.mindstudio.ai/blog/grok-4-6-pricing-access).

* **Frontier performance at a fraction of the cost**: Grok 4.6 keeps Grok 4.5's pricing at $2 per million input tokens and $6 per million output tokens, making it roughly 60% cheaper than GPT-5.6 Sol per token [MindStudio - August 2026](https://www.mindstudio.ai/blog/grok-4-6-pricing-access). Artificial Analysis found the model is also token-efficient, completing its benchmark run at 84 cents per task -- in line with Kimi K3, and 32% cheaper than GPT-5.6 Sol and 73% cheaper than Fable 5.

* **Real-world testing is more mixed than the benchmarks**: Some early testers are calling Grok 4.6 their new default, including Martin Casado of a16z on his technical tests, while others report dangerous mistakes the model later tries to cover up and an over-eager, verbose behavior that values completeness above economics. The sober read circulating in AI circles -- echoed by Nathan Lambert -- is that 4.6 shows xAI is not out of the race but not at the top, sitting middle-top-ish against GPT-5.6 and Fable 5, both of which are several months old and only un-updated because new frontier releases now run through government safety review [Nathan Lambert](https://natolambert.com/).

* **Elon Musk teases Grok 4.7**: Musk tweeted that Grok 4.7 will be "something special," saying he'd be "shocked if any model is better at real-world engineering than 4.7," and that the SpaceX training corpus is unique. The community's usual reflexive skepticism of Musk timelines has softened, though Anthropic has reportedly already shipped Fable 5.5 and is waiting to release it.

* **Cognition seeks $40 billion just three months after pricing at $26 billion**: Bloomberg reports Cognition is in early talks to raise another roughly $1 billion at a $40 billion valuation -- up almost 50% in a quarter -- with sources saying the coding-agent company has doubled its revenue run rate to $1 billion since the last round [Bloomberg - August 12, 2026](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-12/ai-startup-cognition-in-new-funding-talks-at-40-billion-value) [Wikipedia - Cognition AI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scott_Wu). Given that Cursor sought a $50 billion on $2 billion in annualized revenue before SpaceX acquired it in a $60 billion all-stock deal, some investors now think SpaceX's Cursor bargain could look like a steal -- a pitch Cognition (led by CEO Scott Wu) openly dismissed, with a company executive telling investors, "We aren't selling."

* **Lovable raises $400 million and inches from vibe coding toward Shopify**: The $13.3 billion Series C, led by Menlo Ventures and an EQT-managed fund, positions Lovable less as a vibe-coding tool and more as a platform for running businesses, with CEO Anton Osika framing it as the "most intuitive platform to build and run a business" [Reuters - August 12, 2026](https://www.reuters.com/business/media-telecom/vibe-coding-startup-lovable-raises-400-million-133-billion-valuation-2026-08-12/). User survey data cited in the announcement shows nearly 8 in 10 are building a business or side project they hope to monetize, and more than a third of those are already earning revenue.

* **The neoclouds have a line out the door**: CoreWeave doubled revenue year-over-year to $2.6 billion for the quarter, with cash burn also doubling to $5.7 billion per quarter, and reported a $104 billion demand backlog that had grown by $25 billion since end of June [DataCenterDynamics - August 2026](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/neocloud-results-q2-2026-coreweave-nebius-cerebras/). Nebius posted 454% revenue growth to $582 million, beat earnings forecasts by 83%, and said its Blackwell compute auctions cleared 15% above the prior Hopper record price; CEO Arkady Volozh told investors they could sell their entire 2027 capacity today if they wanted. Markets rewarded both: Nebius jumped 34% and CoreWeave 19%.

* **China's capex boom is running the US playbook on a 3-6 month delay**: Tencent tripled AI infrastructure spend to $7.8 billion ($52.8 billion yuan) in the quarter, flipping free cash flow negative as incremental earnings are poured into infrastructure [The Information - August 2026](https://www.theinformation.com/briefings/tencents-capex-nearly-triples-compute-ai-models-tools). Chief Strategy Officer James Mitchell said a "very substantial portion" of new compute is being allocated to Tencent's own models and applications, while President Martin Lau noted they could sell the compute to outside customers if they wanted but are prioritizing internal needs for now -- an attitude strikingly similar to US hyperscaler narratives from Q1.

* **Samsung's AI chip-design gains**: Per Korean reports, three months of integrating Claude Code into its software stack let Samsung cut system-on-chip verification time from three months to two days, and let one second-year engineer complete a month-long task in a single day. The episode frames this as the jagged frontier of enterprise AI: big efficiency on highly customized jobs, and junior employees contributing far beyond their experience.

* **The White House reverses on open models**: Wired reports the administration will expand its model-testing framework to cover open models once they reach the same capabilities as state-of-the-art closed models, after initially planning to exclude them. The logic is pro-open-source: officials worry the framework would otherwise read as a stamp of approval, creating a two-tier system that makes enterprises hesitant to use open models and disincentivizes US labs from shipping them. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent publicly retweeted Zuckerberg's "sustaining US leadership in AI means advancing both open and closed-weight models," while President Trump reportedly insists the framework stay voluntary, believing formal regulation would help China catch up.

* **Don't count Google out while Sergey Brin is pushing resources**: After last week's departures of Google DeepMind CEO Demis Hassabis and longtime senior figure Jeff Dean [StartupHub AI - August 7, 2026](https://www.startuphub.ai/ai-news/ai-figures/2026/figure-demis-hassabis-leadership-transition-2026-08-07), many are writing Google off as a frontier lab. Reuters reports, however, that co-founder Sergey Brin has become a key day-to-day force, telling engineers it's time to play catch-up and using his implicit power as co-founder to push resources toward areas like recursive self-improvement. Google is also reportedly relocating AI training out of DeepMind's London office and back to Mountain View, where Brin can work directly with key researchers and cut through internal bureaucracy.

* **Google reportedly skips ahead to Gemini 4**: Sentiment holds that a merely competent Gemini 3.5 Pro -- already months behind schedule -- would now read as a failure in context. Per leakers cited in the episode, teams are instead shifting toward the scaled-up Gemini 4, a riskier bet that makes more sense given how far behind Google is.

* **DeepSeek V4 Pro's leaked benchmarks look frontier; early reality doesn't**: Hours after Grok 4.6 launched, leaked numbers put the updated DeepSeek V4 Pro at 87.9% on Terminal Bench 2.1 -- within a point or two of Fable 5 and GPT-5.6 Sol -- and ahead on CyberGym [MindStudio - August 2026](https://www.mindstudio.ai/blog/deepseek-v4-pro-0813-benchmarks) [TechTimes - August 13, 2026](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/324241/20260813/deepseek-v4-pro-0813-goes-ga-benchmark-claims-await-independent-proof.htm). But Artificial Analysis scored it just 53 on its index -- barely ahead of V4 Flash and behind Kimi K3 -- and first impressions skew toward "benchmark slop" in real-world testing.

* **In late 2026, the question is where a model fits in the stack, not just raw power**: The argument gaining ground -- echoed by contributors from DAX (OpenCode) and Augustin Lebron -- is that even if Kimi K3, Grok 4.6, and DeepSeek V4 Pro are more benchmark-maxed than Fable and GPT-5.6, it doesn't matter, because they're an order of magnitude cheaper. As the frontier advances, fewer people need the bleeding edge and need it less often.

* **Ramp: businesses found their price ceiling with Fable 5**: Ramp's August AI Index shows Fable 5 at just 6% of Anthropic tokens purchased and 11.4% of dollars spent, versus GPT-5.6 Sol's 25% of OpenAI tokens -- a new upper bound on how much businesses are willing to spend on AI [Ramp AI Index - August 2026](https://ramp.com/data/ai-index-august-2026) [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/fable-5s-slow-adoption-suggests-corporate-willingness-to-pay-for-frontier-ai-has-hit-a-ceiling/). Ramp concludes that more performance is no longer worth the price tag, especially with open models only a few months behind.

* **The Fable 5 'slow adoption' data has two big holes**: First, selection bias: Ramp's numbers come from a corporate spend-management product whose users are literally optimizing away from more-powerful-than-needed models. Second, and more damning: Fable 5 carries a 30-day data retention policy for US government safety checks, and many enterprises simply can't touch that with a long pole -- a detail Ramp's own economist, Ara Kharazian, later amplified with a self-retweet.

**Primary takeaway**: The model landscape is not slowing down -- it's getting crowded. With Grok 4.6 back at the frontier table, multiple credible Chinese and open-weight challengers, order-of-magnitude-cheaper near-frontier options, and Anthropic and OpenAI both sitting on more advanced models they're holding back, the practical reality for buyers is more choice than ever, not less. The question is no longer "which lab is best on the benchmark?" but "which model fits the job you actually need to do, at a price you can actually pay?"
