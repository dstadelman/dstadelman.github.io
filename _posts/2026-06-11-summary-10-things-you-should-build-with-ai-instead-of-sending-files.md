[AI Daily Brief - 10+ Things You Should Build With AI Instead of Sending Files](https://podcasters.spotify.com/pod/show/nlw/episodes/10-Things-You-Should-Build-With-AI-Instead-of-Sending-Files-e3keqlt)

OpenAI's June 2, 2026 announcement of Codex Sites -- a feature that lets Codex output work as interactive, hosted web pages -- sparked this episode's deep dive into why knowledge workers should treat websites as a default artifact format rather than an exception. The host walks through roughly a dozen common workplace documents and explains how AI-built microsites solve systemic problems with each one, from version collapse to distribution friction to audience flattening. The case rests on a broader industry signal: Cloudflare reported in June 2026 that agent and bot browsing had exceeded human browsing for the first time, meaning knowledge artifacts need to work for machines as well as people [https://piunikaweb.com/2026/06/04/cloudflare-bot-traffic-overtakes-humans/].

* **Codex Sites anchors the episode's timeliness**: Launched at OpenAI's "Intelligence at Work" event on June 2, 2026, Sites is a simplified way to take projects built with Codex and publish them as interactive web apps. It's in preview for ChatGPT Business and Enterprise customers, signaling OpenAI's push to make non-developers the primary user base -- non-developer adoption is reportedly three times faster than engineering adoption [https://openai.com/index/codex-for-every-role-tool-workflow/][https://www.buildfastwithai.com/blogs/openai-sites-codex-launch-review-2026].
* **The version/currency problem**: Any downloadable file is a snapshot that immediately goes stale. A URL provides a canonical, continuously updatable home. The host notes this isn't new -- tools like DocSend and Google Docs tried to patch it -- but websites are a more generalist, native solution.
* **Distribution friction is eliminated by links**: Downloadable files require format compatibility, bandwidth, and the recipient's willingness to organize them. A URL passes cleanly through email, Slack, CRM, calendar invites, and newsletters, rendering equally on every device.
* **Navigation constraints of documents vs. websites**: Docs are linear, spreadsheets are tabular, PDFs are paged. Each forces one structure on all readers. Websites let different audiences find their own path -- by topic, role, urgency, or depth -- without reading through a table of contents.
* **Interactive and observability advantages**: Unlike passive PDFs, websites can hold interactivity natively (toggling variables, embedded calculators) and produce behavioral signal (what was clicked, searched, shared, revisited, abandoned). This feedback loop makes the artifact improvable over time.
* **AI-generated website shift**: The core insight is that AI has collapsed the cost of building functional websites to near zero for any "semi-capable" person. This structural change means artifacts that were previously locked in static formats -- slide decks, strategy memos, research reports, sales proposals, spreadsheets, client updates, case studies, competitive analyses, training materials, employee handbooks, board materials, investor updates, recruiting packets, and brand guidelines -- are now all viable as interactive microsites.
* **The agent-consumption angle**: As Cloudflare confirmed that AI agent traffic exceeded human traffic on the web, the host argues that knowledge workers who design their artifacts as properly structured HTML and web pages get a future-proof advantage over those still shipping PDFs and CSVs that agents struggle to parse [https://www.techspot.com/news/112657-bots-have-officially-overtaken-humans-internet-cloudflare.html].
* **Sponsor context**: Episodes is sponsored by Robots & Pencils (AWS pattern partner shipping agentic systems), AssemblyAI (launched a Voice Agent API earlier in 2026), ZeoCoder (ZenoCoder runs zencoder.ai and launched Zenflow Work -- a spec-driven orchestration layer for non-coding workflows), and OutSystems (enterprise agentic systems platform).

The episode's primary argument is structural rather than list-based: websites are not just prettier presentations -- they are a fundamentally different category of knowledge artifact that solves document fatigue, version collapse, audience flattening, and agent-incompatibility all at once. The fact that AI coding tools are now building websites as a default output (rather than code-only) means knowledge workers who haven't started exploring this shift are about to fall behind their peers who have.
