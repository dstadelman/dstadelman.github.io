# Summary: 9 AI Techniques You Probably Haven't Tried

[AI Daily Brief - 9 AI Techniques You Probably Haven't Tried](https://podcasters.spotify.com/pod/show/nlw/episodes/9-AI-Techniques-You-Probably-Havent-Tried-e3nm50u)

Host Nathaniel Whittemore opens with a headline that lands just after Dario Amodei argued that "the thing that will work is actually curing cancer" -- and a day later, Moderna and Merck announced a successful phase III trial for a personalized mRNA cancer vaccine in high-risk melanoma. The episode uses that moment to set up a bigger claim: AI's real value is arriving in unglamorous, ambient forms, not as a single breakthrough. Then it switches into operator mode and walks through nine concrete techniques power users have been experimenting with in the last few weeks -- from Codex live voice mode, to letting AI watch you work, to Claude Tag as a shared team teammate, all the way to local Qwen-3.8-27b running on a laptop.

* **Cancer vaccine clears phase III; the "AI" label is contested:** Moderna and Merck announced INTerpath-001 success for intismeran autogene plus Keytruda in high-risk resected melanoma, with recurrence-free-survival benefits in more than 1,100 patients [source](https://www.merck.com/news/merck-and-moderna-announce-phase-3-interpath-001-trial-of-intismeran-autogene-plus-keytruda-met-endpoints-of-recurrence-free-survival-rfs-and-distant-metastasis-free-survival-dmfs-in-patient/). Whittemore pushes back on the reflex to call it "AI" -- closer to AlphaFold-style ML than a chatbot shortcut -- but also resists dismissing it; Moderna's stock rose ~125% within hours [source](https://aidailybrief.ai/e/2026-08-20).

* **OpenAI ships Private Safety Processing for enterprise API customers:** the technique extends automated safety scanning across an entire session, including customer-controlled stores and multi-step agent traces, while stripping customer data from the summary an OpenAI employee receives if the system flags something [source](https://openai.com/index/offering-zero-data-retention-for-frontier-models/). The bet is that ZDR-grade privacy and frontier-model safety can no longer be a trade-off: false positives no longer leak customer data, and enterprise customers no longer have to give up their ZDR clause to get frontier access.

* **Replit's "Free Mode" routes the $20 plan through GPT-5.6 Luna:** despite the name, Free Mode still bills against paid subs, but routes everyday queries through a lower-capability model that Replit claims produces 30x more work per subscription [source](https://aidailybrief.ai/e/2026-08-20). Whittemore reads this as OpenAI deliberately competing on two axes at once -- the capability frontier (5.6 Sol, Astra) and the efficiency frontier (Luna) -- the latter aimed squarely at Chinese open-weight models that own cost-per-token.

* **SpaceX reportedly tried to buy Cognition -- five days after closing the $60B Cursor deal:** Bloomberg reported that SpaceX approached Cognition about a potential acquisition, possibly including arrangements for Cognition to use SpaceX compute; Cognition last raised at $26B in May and is seeking at least $40B in a new round [source](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/19/cognition-ceo-denies-report-that-spacex-tried-to-acquire-the-startup/). Cognition CEO Scott Wu publicly denied the talks ("not for sale"), and Elon Musk put the matter to bed by saying you should "not actually have to doubt anyone" here -- because what even counts as "approaching someone about a deal" is blurry enough that an offhand conversation can start the process; Whittemore reads the chatter as consistent with a Musk acquisition machine that has not yet sated its appetite.

* **The White House AI safety framework stays closed-door:** sources say the framework was distributed as paper copies to a closed room at the White House, with attendees allowed to take notes but not to keep written versions [source](https://www.cato.org/news-releases/catos-londono-voluntary-unpublished-ai-testing-framework-wont-reassure-anyone). Cato's Juan Londoño called the move a violation of the rule of law -- "a framework that raises more questions than it answers is worse than no framework at all" -- which Whittemore agrees with in full, especially as labs like OpenAI voluntarily slow frontier work.

* **1. Voice mode is doing real work:** Allie K. Miller published a step-by-step setup for using Codex live voice mode from a phone to dispatch tasks while you're walking, cooking, or in a meeting [source](https://x.com/alliekmiller). Her framing -- "the way I'm working looks completely different than a month ago" -- is the pattern that matters: less about a specific use case, more about ambient, push-style interaction with an assistant that can triage, delegate, and report back without you clicking anything.

* **2. Let AI watch you do the job:** ChatGPT's "computer history" feature builds a timeline of what you did and how you did it, and Grok Bot adds an explicit teach-mode button that watches a specific action you perform and turns it into a repeatable agent behavior [source](https://x.ai/news/introducing-grok-bot). The takeaway is that the hardest-to-explain parts of your work no longer need to be in a prompt -- you can just show the agent.

* **3. Build a "no-AI-isms" writing skill:** capture the specific tics you object to -- compressed sentences, "not this, but that" constructions, self-congratulatory caveats -- into a single skill or style guide the LLM reads before drafting [source](https://www.aihero.dev/skills). Other users are feeding in domain-specific standards (aircraft-maintenance ASD-STE-100, for instance); the point is to consolidate your anti-patterns in one place so every subsequent output has to clear the bar.

* **4. Use Claude's /design command:** in Claude Code, `slash design` opens an artboard workflow where you can drop notes on regions, preview style variants, and iterate on micro-decisions rather than rolling a single mega-prompt [source](https://aidailybrief.ai/e/2026-08-20). Whittemore's read: this is the first command where macro exploration and micro-precision both live in the same surface, which is exactly the split that has been missing from AI design tools.

* **5. Treat skill hygiene as a discipline, not a binary:** Matt Pocock has catalogued 25 skills at aihero.dev/skills with a clear "when to use" framing, including "grill-with-docs", an interview-loop skill that writes its vocabulary and hard decisions into the repo as a side effect [source](https://www.aihero.dev/skills). The point is not to install a library -- it's to keep asking which skill fits which step of your flow, and to prune the ones that aren't earning their keep.

* **6. Team agents ("multiplayer AI"):** Claude Tag (beta since June 23, 2026) is one shared Claude identity that lives in a Slack channel, joins as a teammate, and can be steered by any member, with per-channel context and permissions [source](https://stateofsurveillance.org/news/anthropic-claude-tag-slack-agent-coworker-2026/). TenX is publishing on the "citizen SDLC" -- a six-stage lifecycle that moves a single employee's AI-coded prototype into company-wide production [unverified-by-web]. Whittemore's read: this is the biggest trend of the fall, because real work lives in the hands-off between individuals, not inside one person's terminal.

* **7. Grok Bot is the most flexible single-player agent on the market:** launched August 11, each Grok Bot gets its own cloud computer, its own logins, and 24/7 runtime [source](https://x.ai/news/introducing-grok-bot). Whittemore highlights the "chief-of-staff" pattern -- one Grok Bot that delegates to a swarm of specialist Grok Bots -- and notes that a Hermes desktop "bot mode" is offering an open-weights equivalent for those who'd rather not work in the Grok ecosystem.

* **8. Local AI is actually viable now:** Qwen 3.8 27B scores 52 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index on commodity hardware -- a number that would have been state-of-the-art a few months ago [source](https://aidailybrief.ai/e/2026-08-20). Whittemore's pitch: if you've been waiting for a local model that can genuinely carry a workload, this is the moment to actually dive in.

* **9. Short prompts are a superpower:** Allie K. Miller's 18 two-word prompt list -- "now what", "please fix", "simulate it", "plan for edge cases", "and remember this" -- maps one-to-one onto how you'd delegate to a junior [source](https://x.com/alliekmiller). The pattern: the shorter the prompt, the more the agent has to carry, and the more you see whether it actually understands the task.

* **The throughline:** the nine techniques are not a checklist; they're a cross-section of a power user's operating model taking shape. Voice mode turns AI into a collaborator you can interrupt in the hallway; watching your own work turns the hardest parts of a job into teachable behavior; skills turn your objections into standing policy; /design turns one mega-prompt into an editing surface; team agents turn a single terminal into a shared channel; Grok Bot turns a chatbot into an always-on colleague; and Allie's two-word prompts are the smallest unit of the same idea -- give the AI less to carry and let it figure out the rest.

Tie it all together and the episode is really two stories wearing one title. The first is the Moderna/Merck headline: Dario Amodei was right that "the thing that will work is actually curing cancer," and a day later a regulated-industry phase III trial delivered exactly that. The second is that the same compounding of compute, talent, and ML infrastructure that made that trial possible is quietly reorganizing how ordinary knowledge work gets done. One is the future we all keep getting promised; the other is the present we're already living in -- and the nine techniques are the fastest way to catch up.
