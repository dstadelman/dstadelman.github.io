## Summary

Nathaniel Whittemore's AI Daily Brief ran a new condensed format this week -- a five-minute weekly roundup aimed at busy professionals who want the important AI news without the 25-minute daily commitment. The episode covered token efficiency as the defining theme of the week, new tools, and policy developments that could reshape the AI industry. Here's what matters.

* **Token efficiency is the new AI battleground**: The industry has transitioned from the "token subsidy era" (per-seat models like OpenAI and Anthropic's, where subscribers consumed thousands of dollars of tokens) to a "token shortage era" where usage-based pricing dominates. Uber capped employee AI tool usage at $1,500 per month [1], Walmart limited its Code Puppy assistant after demand drove costs through the roof [2], and TSMC's CEO suggested the chip shortage will persist for years, not months [3]. The industry is adapting in real-time.

* **The market is responding to token costs**: Factory (factory.ai) launched native model routing that automatically selects the right model for each task, maintaining frontier performance while cutting costs by 20-25% [4]. Perplexity unveiled a hybrid local-cloud inference system at Computex that routes tasks between device and cloud for better cost and privacy [5]. Harvey partnered with Fireworks AI to build a legal agent using GLM 5.1 as the primary worker with Claude Opus 4.7 as a sparse front-tier advisor -- achieving competitive results on the Harvey LAB benchmark at a fraction of the cost [6]. Microsoft reported that its ma-1 model, post-trained on McKinsey's internal tasks, beat GPT-5.5's performance at one-tenth the cost [7].

* **Codex updates worth exploring**: OpenAI's Codex received an expansion of its plugin ecosystem, new annotations (letting you edit and interact with specific parts of any website or document), and a feature called Sites -- letting you turn anything you build in Codex into a website or web app with one click. Role-specific plugin packs (sales, data science, etc.) are now available. Sites is currently restricted to business and enterprise users. [8]

* **Policy discourse is accelerating**: Bernie Sanders published an op-ed in the New York Times (May 26) announcing the American A.I. Sovereign Wealth Fund Act, which would create a sovereign wealth fund giving the public a 50% ownership stake in major AI labs [9]. Anthropic and OpenAI both published policy papers this week flagging early signs of recursive self-improvement in current systems [10]. The conversation around government ownership of AI companies is shifting rapidly.

* **What's coming next**: SpaceX is expected to price its IPO on June 11 with trading to begin June 12, targeting a $1.75 trillion valuation for what is projected to be the largest IPO in history [11].

The episode frames 2026 as the year token economics finally forces every organization -- from Fortune 500 companies to solo operators -- to build real systems around model selection, context management, and cost-conscious agent workflows. The cost equation is no longer theoretical; it's already constraining how companies deploy AI in practice.
