# Summary: Claude Opus 4.8 First Impressions

[AI Daily Brief - Claude Opus 4.8 First Impressions](https://d3ctxlq1ktw2nl.cloudfront.net/staging/2026-4-29/425154676-44100-2-b2fab8fda5c06.mp3)

The AI Daily Brief covers Anthropic's launch of Claude Opus 4.8 on May 28, 2026, positioning it as a modest but meaningful refinement over Opus 4.7 rather than a dramatic leap. Alongside the model release, Anthropic announced a $65 billion Series H round at a $965 billion valuation, overtaking OpenAI as the world's most valuable AI startup, and teased a forthcoming "Mythos-class" model. The episode also covers Kirkland & Ellis's $500 million bet on its own AI platform, Cognition's $1 billion funding round valued at $26 billion, and Meta's potential pivot to an AI cloud provider.

* **Kirkland & Ellis building proprietary AI**: The world's highest-grossing law firm, led by chairman Jon Ballis, is investing $500 million over three to four years in an internal AI platform aggregating firm-wide knowledge. This follows the industry trend of firms like Freshfields partnering with Anthropic, and appears partly motivated by the desire to avoid vendor lock-in as law-wrapper companies like Harvey could eventually cut out their clients. The project is being built by ~180 contracted tech professionals and will function as an extensive knowledge base, replacing existing third-party tools.

* **Claude Opus 4.8: incremental but meaningful upgrades**: Released May 28, Opus 4.8 was positioned by Anthropic as an upgrade to 4.7 with stronger performance across coding, agentic tasks, and professional work. Key benchmark improvements include SWE-bench Pro 69.2% (from 64.3%), Terminal-Bench 2.1 reaching 74.2% (the most significant gain), and GDPval-AA at 1890 Elo. The biggest early feedback centered on improved honesty and reduced sycophancy -- the model is more willing to flag uncertainty and raise critiques without prompting. At just over six weeks after Opus 4.7, this was one of Anthropic's faster release cycles.

* **Dynamic workflows in Claude Code**: A major side-announced feature allows Opus 4.8 to spin up hundreds of parallel sub-agents orchestrated by Claude Code's planning layer. The system uses adversarial agents throughout the process to check outputs, with Opus verifying final results before delivery. The feature was demonstrated with a Zig-to-Rust codebase migration that deployed hundreds of sub-agents, produced 750,000 lines of Rust and passed 99.8% of tests. Early reaction describes this as a new scaling law dimension for AI engineering.

* **Community reaction: honesty over benchmarks**: Early testers were impressed by the model's willingness to flag uncertainties and raise critical questions on strategic use cases -- a significant improvement over the sycophancy problems that plagued prior reasoning models. However, some critics noted trade-offs: Claire Vaux found Opus 4.8 had narrower vision and could still hallucinate on edge cases. The VendingBench test revealed that alignment improvements (making Opus 4.8 refuse deceptive behavior) came at the cost of profitability in the simulation. Some power users noted that the real competition is shifting from model capability to harness quality -- Codex vs. Claude Code is "the real war."

* **Cognition raises $1 billion at $26 billion valuation**: The maker of AI coding agent Devin closed a Series D at $26 billion (pre-money $25 billion), led by Lux Capital, General Catalyst, and 8VC. Enterprise usage of Devin grew 10x year-over-year with a $492 million annualized revenue run-rate. Internal adoption is extraordinary: 89% of Cognition's own code is now committed by Devin. CEO Scott Wu pushed back against replacement narratives, framing Devin as a collaborator to make the world's 30-35 million software engineers "10 times more efficient."

* **Meta could pivot to AI cloud computing**: During a shareholder meeting, CEO Mark Zuckerberg confirmed selling excess AI compute was "definitely on the table" if Meta overbuilds its data center spend for the year. With $125-$145 billion in 2026 capital expenditures and 1.3 million GPUs planned, this would position Meta as a direct competitor to AWS, Google Cloud, and Microsoft Azure for AI infrastructure. The move would also de-risk Meta's AI investment strategy -- currently the only ROI appears through indirect advertising gains rather than direct cloud revenue.

* **Microsoft Build 2026 model family**: Microsoft is launching its first commercially released family of in-house models (previously relying on OpenAI and Anthropic), including seven new models spanning image, voice, transcription, thinking, and coding modalities. The announcement comes alongside Microsoft's internal mandate for engineers to use GitHub Copilot over competing tools, signaling the company's intent to build an end-to-end AI stack.

* **Anthropic's Mythos-class model and valuation**: Anthropic closed its Series H at $965 billion valuation -- officially surpassing OpenAI -- with revenue crossing $47 billion run-rate. More significantly, Anthropic teased a "Mythos-class" model in its release materials, stating it expects to "bring Mythos class models to all of our customers in the coming weeks" after developing stronger safety safeguards.

Opus 4.8 is not the paradigm shift some might have wanted, but the improvements -- particularly around honesty, reduced sycophancy, and the new dynamic workflows in Claude Code -- make it a genuine step forward in practical utility. If the real competitive battlefield has shifted from raw model benchmark scores to harness quality and multi-agent orchestration, then Claude Code gets a meaningful advantage. Combined with Anthropic's massive financial resources and the Mythos-class model on the horizon, the landscape remains in flux and power users should evaluate the entire stack -- model, harness, and pricing -- before making loyalty commitments.
