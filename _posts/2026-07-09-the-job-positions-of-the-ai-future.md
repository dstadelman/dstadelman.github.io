# Summary: The Job Positions of the AI Future

![AI Daily Brief](https://podcasts.files.nr-assets.net/podcast/image-type/3000/1680633614/the-ai-daily-brief-formerly-the-ai-breakdown.jpg)

[Nathaniel Whittemore - The Job Positions of the AI Future](https://podcasters.spotify.com/pod/show/nlw/episodes/The-Job-Positions-of-the-AI-Future-e3lm4ub)

Drawing on Claude Code creator Boris Cherny's framework and expanding it beyond engineering, this episode explores how agentic AI is reshaping job roles into functional archetypes rather than traditional titles. The host applies these patterns across product, engineering, sales, marketing, and back-office teams to offer a new lens for thinking about organizational design in the AI era.

* **Five work-facing roles**: Cherny's archetypes -- prototyper (generates many ideas), builder (ships prototypes into production-grade infrastructure), sweeper (optimizes UX, code, and system architecture), grower (iterates toward product-market fit), and maintainer (keeps mature systems secure, reliable, and efficient) -- represent a product lifecycle that transcends job titles. Engineering, design, and PMs all span these roles depending on where the team sits in its maturity curve.
* **Missing externally-facing archetypes**: The host adds six external-facing roles Cherny's framework omits: editor (decides which prototypes deserve investment), scout (aggregates market signal from the real world into the organization), evangelist (gets the market to see the product the way builders do), orchestrator (manages how disparate role bundles fit across the org), conductor (manages agent teams as synthetic workers), and risk steward (anticipates what could derail fast-moving agentic workflows before it happens).
* **Sales application**: Prototyper = testing new pitches/segments; Builder = turning winners into scalable playbooks; Sweeper = pruning dead scripts and bag-fit segments; Grower = expanding what's working based on customer interaction; Maintainer = building pipeline discipline and account coverage systems that persist quarter-to-quarter. The editor, scout, and risk steward add particular value when sales motions push too hard.
* **Marketing parallels**: Scout reads the audience and competitor moves; prototyper tests new narratives and channels; editor uses brand taste to select which angles ship; builder converts sparks into campaign machines; sweeper cuts weak messaging and channel bloat; grower obsesses over conversion data; maintainer protects brand alignment and lifecycle systems like CRM stack hygiene.
* **Back-office implications**: Finance, HR, and ops may have fewer prototyper/builder ratios and more maintainers -- consistent with their role as the "core functions" of the org. However, agentic tools are pushing product thinking deeper into back office: a finance person can now prototype custom expense-reporting workflows themselves instead of waiting months for IT tickets. Making is becoming the ultimate career hedge in any function.
* **The core shift**: Underlying everything is access to agents -- vanishingly cheap code generation means every function starts growing its own maker. Thinking in terms of personality-based archetypes rather than rigid job descriptions gives individuals and organizations a language for navigating this organizational transition as it happens.

Summary: The episode argues that agentic AI isn't just replacing tasks -- it's revealing functional archetypes in work that cut across traditional departments. Organizations that start mapping their teams to these roles (prototyper through maintainer, plus editor/scout/evangelist/orchestrator/conductor/risk steward) will be better positioned to intentionally shape the organizational change already underway.
