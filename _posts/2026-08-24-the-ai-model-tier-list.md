# Summary: The AI Model Tier List

[The AI Daily Brief - The AI Model Tier List](https://podcasters.spotify.com/pod/show/nlw/episodes/The-AI-Model-Tier-List-e3nr9cb)

Following a viral AI-model tier list, The AI Daily Brief ties that debate to the week's bigger story: enterprises are no longer choosing one "best" model but assembling multi-model stacks that route each task to the best-fit model -- with open-weight models now serving a fast-growing share of real workloads. The headlines run from Hugging Face reportedly seeking a $13B exit and NVIDIA's $6B licensing-plus-talent grab of coding-model startup Poolside, to NVIDIA's 17% top-end chip price hike, Alibaba's record $10B-plus share sale, and Unitree's 460% humanoid-robot IPO pop. The main episode argues the real shift is structural -- AT&T serving 40% of employee queries with open models, Vercel's gateway flipping to 62% open tokens, and a chorus of investors predicting frontier labs keep the economic value while open models take the volume.

* **The model war is now about the stack**: The episode's through-line is the shift from "which model is best?" to "how do you route the right task to the right model?" now that frontier usage, cost, and efficiency all matter for individuals, teams, and enterprises.
* **Hugging Face shopping a $13B exit**: Business Insider reports Hugging Face engaged an investment bank to field offers near a $13B price -- nearly 3x its 2023 round at $4.5B (backed by Google, Amazon, Nvidia, Intel, and Salesforce); commentators frame a buyer as acquiring the distribution and coordination layer around open models, the same logic behind Stripe's $7B purchase of router OpenRouter.
* **NVIDIA's $6B Poolside deal**: Eric Newcomer reported that Nvidia struck a non-exclusive $6B licensing deal for Poolside's technology plus a $1B equity investment at a $12B valuation, hiring 100+ of Poolside's engineers to advance its Nemotron models; Poolside says it is "not an acquisition and not an acqui-hire," with the founders staying to keep AGI "a technology built by many, in the open."
* **NVIDIA assembling the whole open-model stack**: The show connects the Poolside deal to a reported stake in data-labeling firm Mercor (which Nvidia used for RL on its last two Nemotron models), a reported Perplexity investment at a $30B valuation, and the broader push -- reading it as Nvidia moving across research, talent, training data, and the app layer.
* **NVIDIA's 17% chip price hike**: The Information reports top-end Grace Blackwell and Vera Rubin chips rising as much as 17%, even on units already ordered for delivery next year, with a 72-GPU Vera Rubin rack heading toward $8M -- roughly $5B more per gigawatt of compute -- as memory-shortage costs are expected to be passed on.
* **Alibaba's record $10B+ share sale**: Alibaba completed the largest follow-on offering in the Hong Kong market (~$10.2B, 710M shares at HK$112.7) to fund its AI build-out; shares fell as much as 10% Monday, with Union Bancaire Privee's Vey-Sern Ling noting it signals a rush to stay ahead on AI capex, while Michael Burry praised the company's low-cost LLM advance but warned against blessing the share issuance.
* **Unitree's humanoid-robot IPO**: Unitree Robotics raised ~$900M on the Shanghai exchange, debuting at a $9B market cap and surging more than 460% on day one -- the fourth Chinese IPO this year to pop past 400% -- a signal of demand in the embodied-AI sector.
* **Dr. Dre and Jimmy Iovine on AI in music**: In a New York Times profile, Dr. Dre and longtime producer Jimmy Iovine call AI "a new tool for creativity," not a threat -- Dre compares the backlash to early resistance to the drum machine and says those who see it as a threat "have trouble creating," and both note many producers already use AI but won't admit it.
* **Theo's tier list, top to bottom**: Content creator Theo put Fable 5 alone in S tier, GPT-5.6 Sol in A, Kimi K3 / DeepSeek V4 Flash / GPT-5.6 Luna in B, Grok 4.6 and Meta's Muse Spark 1.2 in C, Anthropic's Opus 5 and Sonnet 5 plus GPT-5.6 Terra and Cursor's Composer 2.5 in D, DeepSeek V4 Pro in F, and a dedicated "Google tier" below F for Gemini 3.7 Flash and Gemini 3.1 Pro.
* **Theo's Fable-versus-Sol tension**: He rates Fable 5 the single best model -- "the genius everyone avoids working with but no one will fire" whose code he wants to merge and trusts to double-check everything -- yet says GPT-5.6 Sol is the one he'd keep by default, a "slightly dumber robot that does exactly what you tell it," mirroring the host's own run-both-then-commit workflow.
* **Luna over Terra, and price intuition**: Theo ranks Luna two tiers above the "theoretically balanced" Terra -- calling Luna the model he calls the most ("smart, fast, and good at a bunch of random stuff" for non-reversible work) while Terra "makes sense on a pricing chart but not in reality" -- and flags that open-weight Kimi K3 actually costs slightly more than Sol because Sol does more with fewer tokens.
* **The disputed FT chart**: A Financial Times chart showing limited enterprise spend on Fable 5 was read as businesses rejecting the price, but the show's point is that the real driver is Fable 5's 30-day data-retention policy -- a provisional safeguard from its post-shutdown relaunch -- enough for many enterprises to say "absolutely not" regardless of capability.
* **The Ramp data's blind spots**: The chart draws from Ramp's AI Index (put out by lead economist Ara Kharazian), but it comes specifically from Ramp's token-and-cost-control product among already tech-forward users -- a selection bias -- and it misses the glacial pace of enterprise adoption, where many teams still run roughly nine-month-old models.
* **AT&T's open-model migration**: Per The Information, AT&T is holding spend with OpenAI and Anthropic flat while routing ever more traffic to open models -- already serving 40% of employee queries across ~100,000 staff with a 60-70% target -- using Nemotron plus open models from Meta and Google, where a model router cut its AI-coding cost as much as 56% for a ~2% quality drop and self-hosting on Nvidia/AMD chips beat renting cloud.
* **Vercel's gateway flipped in two months**: Vercel CEO Guillermo Rauch showed open-model tokens rising from ~28% to ~62% of AI-gateway traffic (closed falling ~72% to ~38%) between late June and late August -- a developer-skewed shift, but one he and others read as a signal of where the winds are blowing.
* **Where the stack splits**: Investor Gavin Baker predicts closed frontier models end up as 60-90% of economic value but only 15-25% of tokens (and an open-source token costs just as much compute to produce -- bullish for infrastructure), while MIT's Christian Catalini splits the market three ways: commodity open generalists, state-of-the-art closed generalists, and "state-of-the-art specialists" pairing open weights with proprietary context -- the thesis behind Microsoft Foundry and its MAI models.
* **What's coming**: Chatter points to a Fable 5.1 release coming shortly, while OpenAI's Astra -- its next major model family -- has reportedly slipped to September.

The unifying insight is that the question is no longer "which model is the best," but "how do I put the right models together, route each workload to the right one, and stay within data-retention, cost, and reliability constraints?" In that world a tier list is less a ranking than a snapshot of a maturing discipline -- the state-of-the-art lead still matters, but so does the composition of the stack that makes it usable at scale.
