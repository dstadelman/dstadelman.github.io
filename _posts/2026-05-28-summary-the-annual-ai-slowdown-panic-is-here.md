[AI Daily Brief - The Annual AI Slowdown Panic is Here](https://aidailybrief.beehiiv.com/p/the-annual-ai-slowdown-panic-is-here)

The AI Daily Brief examines how the familiar summer "AI slowdown" narrative is resurfacing in 2026 as the industry transitions from a subsidy era to a trade-offs era, where token costs are finally catching up to the massive agentic AI experiment of early 2026. The episode ties together a new rigorous coding benchmark called DeepSWE, leadership tone shifts from Sam Altman and Goldman Sachs on AI job disruption, and a surge in inference-layer startup funding that signals where the market's money is going.

* **DeepSWE benchmark from DataCurve exposes real model divergence**: DataCurve CEO Serena Ge introduced DeepSWE, a long-horizon agentic coding benchmark designed to separate models that can handle real engineering work from those that have simply memorized existing GitHub issues. On DeepSWE, GPT-5.5 leads with 70%, followed by GPT-5.4 at 56% and Opus 4.7 at 54%. The benchmark's spread is dramatically wider than public leaderboards like SWE-Bench, which cluster top models closely together. Chinese models trail significantly -- Kimi K2.6 scored 24% and DeepSeek V4 just 8%. DataCurve's qualitative analysis found that leading models like GPT-5.4 and Opus 4.7 wrote their own verification tests over 80% of the time, while Claude frequently missed multi-part prompt requirements. GPT-5.5 also used roughly half as many tokens as Opus 4.7 with half the runtime and a third of the cost.
* **AI leadership rhetoric is shifting on job displacement**: Sam Altman acknowledged that the industry "miscalculated how easily people could be replaced by computers," saying he expected more impact on entry-level white-collar jobs by now. Goldman Sachs CEO David M. Solomon published a New York Times op-ed calling the AI job apocalypse concern overblown, noting that AI like previous technological revolutions will create more jobs than it destroys. The Atlantic's Derek Thompson and others have pointed out that surging prices for AI compute -- which means demand is outrunning supply -- are the strongest evidence against any genuine slowdown.
* **Inference-layer startups are raising at unicorn speeds**: Baseten (branded BASE10, base10.vc) is closing a $1 billion round at an $11 billion valuation, more than doubling from its $5 billion valuation just three months prior. Annualized revenue tripled from $200 million to $600 million in Q1, with a 20x run-rate increase since March 2025. OpenRouter hit unicorn status with a $113 million Series B led by CapitalG, valuing the company at $1.3 billion. OpenRouter is now serving 100 trillion tokens per month -- a 5x increase in six months.
* **The token crunch is real but misunderstood**: Companies like Uber burned through annual token budgets in four months, while others are shifting from seat-based to usage-based pricing. The U.S. White House even opposed Anthropic's expansion of its most powerful model partly because it wanted priority access to those tokens. Research firm Epic AI estimates global token demand is growing 10x per year while inference capacity is only tripling. Cursor's new Composer 2.5 model (released May 18, 2026) reached third place on Artificial Analysis's Coding Agent Index at a fraction of the cost of Opus 4.7 Max and GPT-5.5 Extra High, showing cheap alternatives are emerging.
* **The summer panic is predictable and ultimately hollow**: The episode traces the annual pattern -- 2023's ChatGPT engagement drop, 2024's training wall fears, and 2025's MIT "95% of AI projects fail" narrative -- all of which were debunked within months by actual breakthroughs. The current narrative claims "vibe-coded apps aren't making money so the bubble will pop," which is the latest version of AI deniers' playbook. The author argues the slowdown is actually healthy because it forces sustainable pricing that better reflects compute costs and reduces the risk of a larger bubble later.

The core message is that the current moment of reckoning -- expensive tokens, shifting pricing models, and the end of the free-agents experiment -- should be framed as a necessary maturation of the AI industry, not a signal of collapse. The companies and leaders who treat this period as an opportunity to refine their adoption strategy, rather than doubling down on the "apocalypse" narrative, will be far ahead when the next growth wave begins.
