# Summary: How to Help People Thrive with AI

[AI Daily Brief - How to Help People Thrive with AI](https://podcasters.spotify.com/pod/show/nlw/episodes/How-to-Help-People-Thrive-with-AI-e3lvj3p)

Nathaniel Whittemore's latest AI Daily Brief examines why AI adoption in organizations remains stuck despite heavy investment, drawing on a recent Atlantic essay by David Brooks and real-world examples like Uber's "Agentic Pods" program. The episode explores the gap between companies rolling out AI tools and workers actually knowing how to use them for meaningful change.

- **Brooks on cognitive effort as the key differentiator**: In his Atlantic essay "The People Who Will Thrive in the AI Age," David Brooks argues that what separates people who thrive from those who don't won't be intelligence but their relationship to mental effort. [unverified -- no web source found] Researchers from ActiveTrack studied 10,000+ workers and found AI adopters' work became more intense, not easier: email/messaging time doubled, business software use rose 94%, focused uninterrupted work fell 9%, and workers took on new tasks rather than doing less. The study is [unverified]. Researchers at UC Berkeley's Haas School of Business similarly found workers using AI began taking on tasks previously outsourced to others -- because coding and engineering became easier, they did those tasks themselves, squeezing work into evenings, weekends, and everywhere in between. The ActiveTrack finding also [unverified].

- **Section's AI proficiency report reveals a massive adoption gap**: A new Section AI proficiency report found that while 69% of surveyed workers said their organization took action on AI agents, only 16% actually use an agent tool at work, and fewer than 10% could define what an AI agent is. Only 30% received agent training. [unverified]

- **MIT research warns of cognitive atrophy**: Brooks cites MIT Media Lab research showing brain connectivity drops as much as 55% when using ChatGPT for similar tasks, and a study from Possibility Sciences finding gamma wave activity -- a marker of cognitive effort -- dropped roughly 40% during AI use. [unverified]

- **Brooks' three AI archetypes**: He identifies "productive passengers" (low need for cognition, use AI to do less), "reluctant optimizers" (medium need try not to rely too heavily but fail in stress), and "mental marathoners" (high need for cognition, like Brooks suggests are the only ones who will thrive). The episode's host disagrees with Brooks' pessimism, arguing we simply haven't asked much of people or challenged them for a long time. The Rivendell school principal Chris Seiban [unverified] is cited as illustrating how students now ask "why do that effort when AI could do it in five minutes."

- **Uber's Agentic Pods experiment**: Uber CTO Praveen Nepali [confirmed name] shared their program of pairing 30+ AI-proficient engineers with domain experts from non-engineering functions (finance, legal, marketing, HR) into two-week "Agentic Pods." In just two months, 16 pods ran across 16 functions with results including capital allocation in 150 cities dropping from 15 hours to 30 minutes, financial pacing reports from 2 days to 10 minutes, and marketing QA from 2 weeks to 50 minutes. [unverified] The bigger insight: the biggest wins came not from automating tasks but redesigning entire workflows -- eliminating handoffs, removing unnecessary approvals, replacing legacy tooling.

- **AI Champions as internal change agents**: The Wall Street Journal's CIO Journal profiled "AI champions" -- super-fans within companies who volunteer for early access and special training to promote adoption. The episode's host argues the real value isn't telling people how good AI is but showing what they could actually do with it. He coined the term "internally deployed vibe coders" as an evolution of this champion concept: people who pair with business functions not just to make existing work 20% faster, but to fundamentally rethink how those functions operate.

The primary takeaway: Productivity gains from AI are real but transient if organizations don't build institutional structures -- like pairing engineers with domain experts -- that teach people new ways of thinking. The reinvestment of saved time is what actually changes a business, not the productivity itself. Organizations do well at AI by building structures that help people discover what's possible, not just making current work faster.
