# Summary: Introducing How We Got Here -- NLW's new podcast

[AI Daily Brief - Introducing How We Got Here -- NLW's new podcast](https://podcasters.spotify.com/pod/show/nlw/episodes/Introducing-How-We-Got-Here----NLWs-new-podcast-e3r45fc)

NLW (Nathaniel Whittemore) is launching a new weekly podcast, How We Got Here, born directly out of making the AI Daily Brief: topics whose backstory is too big to fit a daily episode. The show traces today's most important trends, events, and phenomena back through the economics, politics, technology, and culture that produced them. The premiere episode, "How Tech Lost America," opens with the whiplash reversal on data centers and then goes back 15 years to explain how tech went from America's most trusted industry to a symbol of people's anxieties about money and power.

* **The premise**: many modern criticisms rest on the feeling that the way things are was inevitable, when it was actually the outcome of a set of decisions -- and understanding those decisions reveals more agency to shape what comes next.
* **The Shapiro whiplash**: in June 2025, Pennsylvania Governor Josh Shapiro announced Amazon's $20 billion data center investment, the largest private-sector capital investment in Commonwealth history, promising thousands of jobs and vowing to "ensure that the future of AI runs right through Pennsylvania." Just 14 months later, on August 18, 2026, he signed an executive order implementing "the strictest standards in the nation" for AI data centers, saying he would not allow Pennsylvanians to be "bullied by greedy developers and bulldozed by the lawyers working for these big tech companies."
* **The polling collapse**: a Heatmap Pro poll (conducted by Embold Research) found Americans essentially tied on a local data center in August 2025 (43% support vs 42% opposed), 51% opposed by February 2026, 70% opposed by May, and 75% opposed by August 2026, with more than 60% strongly opposed. Heatmap founding executive editor Robinson Meyer: "Americans have swung a remarkable 33 points against data centers. It's a faster and deeper shift in American public opinion than I would have once thought possible on any issue."
* **Bipartisan third rail**: in the Ohio Senate race, the NRSC -- the Senate GOP's campaign arm -- sent a memo ("Ohio Data Center Risk") not just to Ohio staffers but to the key tech companies and hyperscalers, warning data centers were "the anchor hanging around" Sen. Jon Husted's neck against Sherrod Brown and could lose them the seat.
* **Even the base has turned**: President Trump has pushed back -- arguing data centers are good for communities and rebranding AI as "SI," superintelligence -- but an Economist/YouGov poll in August 2026 found 47% of 2024 Trump voters opposed a data center near them vs 35% who supported one.
* **The deeper story -- inherited anger**: generative AI entered public consciousness with a handicap handed down from earlier tech platforms and leaders. In 2013 Edelman's Trust Barometer rated technology the most trusted industry at 77% (it was #1 the year before too). In 2015, 71% of Americans told Pew that tech companies had a positive effect on the country; by 2019 that was down to 50%, with both parties' attitudes cratering. Gallup's computer-industry rating peaked at 75% positive in 2017 and is down to 48% this year; the internet industry went from 59% positive in 2017 to net negative by 2021 (35% positive vs 40% negative).
* **Show logistics**: new episodes every Monday, roughly 45-60 minutes each, with a fully illustrated companion essay; episode 2 is about how stuff got cheap while life got expensive. Follow, rate, and review at launch: Apple Podcasts and Spotify (links in show notes), companion essays at howwegothere.com.

The core argument of the launch: the data center backlash that flipped a pro-AI governor in 14 months and swung the public 33 points in a year is not an AI-industry problem alone -- it is the latest chapter of a 15-year collapse in trust that tech brought on itself, and the anger arriving with generative AI was inherited. How We Got Here exists to tell those longer stories, on the theory that seeing the decisions behind the present restores a sense that the future is still a set of decisions too.
