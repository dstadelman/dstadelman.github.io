# The Fable 5 Crisis Continues

[AI Daily Brief - The Fable 5 Crisis Continues](https://aidailybrief.ai/e/2026-06-15)

The second day of the Anthropic-vs-White House standoff over Fable 5 grinds into Monday with no resolution, as dueling press releases, a jailbreak report from Amazon, and a wellness-retreat meme fuel an escalating political fire. NLW walks through the competing narratives, the key players on both sides, and the dawning realization that this fight was never going to be settled by engineers.

* **The export control saga**: On June 9, Anthropic launched Claude Fable 5 (and the unrestricted Myths 5 for trusted partners). Three days later, the US Commerce Department imposed an export control ban on all foreign national access to both models. Anthropic immediately disabled them globally.
* **Amazon's jailbreak report**: Axios and multiple outlets identified Amazon as the "trusted partner" that reported a jailbreak of Fable 5's guardrails to the White House Thursday night. Amazon researchers reportedly made Fable 5 discuss security bugs in at least four software platforms. Andrew Morris of Grey Noise Intelligence (sourced by WSJ) characterized this as "a long way from dangerous cybersecurity information" -- noting that Fable's guardrails were so broad they blocked even basic questions like "what is mitochondria."
* **The decision-making process**: WSJ traces the ban to conversations between Amazon CEO Andy Jassy and Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent. White House National Cyber Director Sean Carrancross was also deeply involved, as was Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick. White House Chief of Staff Susie Wiles participated. At least five other tech companies called the White House, but reporting suggests the ban rested almost entirely on Amazon's report.
* **Dueling accounts of the confrontation**: The White House says it asked Dario Amodei to fix the jailbreak or de-deploy the model, and he refused. Anthropic disputes this -- saying it received a 90-minute shutdown order with no details about the nature of the threat. Amodei later had three phone calls with White House and Commerce officials, arguing the jailbreak was specific and narrow, not universal.
* **The wellness-retreat meme**: White House sources said Amodei was unreachable because he was at a "wellness retreat." Ashlee Vance, the Bloomberg journalist who was inside Anthropic's headquarters that day, flatly denied this. Tech reporter Jeff Cafe called it a "linguistic kill shot" -- a sticky, image-generating idea no one can unsee, with Dario pictured in a bathrobe with cucumbers on his eyes.
* **David Sacks' intervention**: Former AI czar David Sacks published a lengthy public post framing Anthropic as the holdout in the crisis, writing that the administration felt the issue "should be easily resolved" and "the ball is in Anthropic's court." Several analysts read Sacks' post as a press release, and some argued it was setting Dario up as a potential sacrificial lamb -- since Sacks specifically shifted from talking about "Anthropic" to naming Dario personally for the first time.
* **Critics of the shutdown**: Eric Voorhees (Venice AI founder) argued the jailbreak "wasn't super serious, and the government used the opportunity to punish and humiliate Anthropic." Software engineer Corey Ward said there's "no safety issue demonstrated by this example." AI policy researcher Miles Brundage wrote that "no one in government who knew what was going on was looped in."
* **The cybersecurity open letter**: Former Facebook CSO Alex Stamos published an open letter (hosted at freefable.org) with 76 cybersecurity experts urging the administration to lift the export controls. Katie Moussouris of Luta Security was among the signatories.
* **Stratechery's analysis**: Ben Thompson's long post "Anthropic's Safety Superpower" argues that the things people criticize about Fable come down to safety, and that the real problem is Anthropic's belief that it's the only entity with sufficient concern about AI danger -- an attitude that "excuses decision after decision, policy after policy, and confrontation after confrontation."
* **The political context**: Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth has been in a longstanding feud with Anthropic after Dario refused to open Claude's safety guardrails. The Fable 5 shutdown came after Hegseth previously branded Anthropic a national security risk, which led to a public relations backlash. The Washington Insider publication also reported that China-linked group concerns factored into the White House's reasoning -- though an Anthropic spokesperson said China was never raised, and Anthropic models are already blocked in China.

The core tension of this story transcends technical arguments about jailbreaks and guardrails. The administration felt Anthropic wasn't taking the issue seriously; Anthropic thought it could reason the White House into agreement. It can't. Anthropic is no longer a scrappy startup -- it's one of two leaders in the most consequential industry of the era, and it has to play ball with the government it has, not the one it wishes it had.
