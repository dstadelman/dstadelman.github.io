## Summary: What the Pope Actually Said About AI

**AI Daily Brief** · May 26, 2026
Podcast: [AI Daily Brief – "What the Pope Actually Said About AI"](https://podcasters.spotify.com/pod/show/nlw/episodes/What-the-Pope-Actually-Said-About-AI-e3jucn5)

### Headlines

**Anthropic's Project Glasswing Mythos update.** Anthropic's limited rollout of its Mythos model to ~50 partners via "Project Glasswing" has identified over 10,000 high-or-critical-severity software vulnerabilities. Most partners found hundreds of severe bugs in just over a month. Testing on open-source repos showed a false-positive rate under 10%. Box CEO Aaron Levie noted the bottleneck has shifted from finding vulnerabilities to verifying and patching them, calling it "a microcosm for the deployment of AI tools more generally." Mozilla reported finding 271 vulnerabilities via its own efforts—more than 10x what it found using Opus 4.6—and Palo Alto Networks released five times as many patches as Mythos. Some finance firms are using Mythos for real-time fraud detection; one case prevented a $1.5M wire transfer to a threat actor.

The exclusivity has frustrated partners and policymakers alike. European, UK, Canadian, and Asian governments have pressed Anthropic to make Mythos available to their financial sectors. The White House approved a secret $9B budget request from the CIA and NSA for their own inference cluster using NVIDIA Blackwell chips. The White House push back to the NYT was sharply worded, calling the reporting "leaked to reporters and repackaged through selectively sourced, unverified claims designed to drive headlines rather than truth." NSA and CIA are reportedly more comfortable with Anthropic's guardrail restrictions than the Pentagon, which had designated Anthropic a supply-chain risk over its limits on autonomous weaponry and domestic surveillance use.

**DeepSeek secures massive funding.** DeepSeek's V4 model runs at roughly $0.44/M input tokens and $0.87/M output tokens—about the cost of Opus 4.5 for a quarter the price of GPT-5. It is pursuing a $10B funding round at a $45B valuation, with the Chinese government's AI investment fund, Tencent, and JD.com as participants. Founder Liang Wenfeng says the focus remains on open-source models and AGI, not monetization. Bloomberg analysts note the "industrial approach to AI is being fueled by a surge in one-person firms using tokens to raise productivity."

**Grok V9-Medium training complete.** Elon Musk announced that Grock V9-Medium, a 1.5-trillion-parameter foundation model, completed training with "evals look good." It includes supplementary cursor-data training, with fine-tuning underway and public release in 2-3 weeks. XAI also rolled out Grok Build to all Grok and X Premium users.

### Pope Leo XIV's *Magnifica Humanitas*

The bulk of the episode covers Pope Leo XIV's first encyclical, *Magnifica Humanitas: On Safeguarding the Human Person in the Time of Artificial Intelligence*. The host calls this the "central theme" of Leo's pontificate—he was elected in May 2025, named himself after Pope Leo XIII (who addressed labor during the Industrial Revolution via *Rerum Novarum* in 1891), and is the first American pope as well as one of the first to use modern technology casually (cell phone, Apple Watch, cable TV).

**Key takeaway:** Leo XIV isn't designating AI as categorically good or bad. He frames AI as a "pivot point in both history and in faith"—a preparatory planting of flags for the debates to come. His central argument, per the host: "human value cannot be reduced to an intelligence benchmark." Even if AI becomes "smarter" than us, it remains categorically different. Paragraph 99 asserts that AI "does not undergo experiences, does not possess a body, does not feel joy or pain, does not mature through relationships," and does not know what love or work mean from within. It simulates but does not understand. This is contra-transhumanist but "not going to be a controversial take among the vast majority of people."

**Key tensions explored:**

- **Jobs and dignity:** The Pope writes that "the pursuit of greater profits cannot justify choices that systematically sacrifice jobs because the human person is an end, not a means." Blake Scholl called this a bad take, arguing tech revolutions naturally eliminate some jobs while creating others. The host notes this echoes the profit-vs-dignity tension already at the core of markets.

- **New colonialism/data mining:** The encyclical recognizes that personal data has become "power to mine" and warns against the potential for a new form of colonialism where those who control data can decide "before others, to whom medicines, investments, and protections will be allocated." More warning for tomorrow than indictment of today.

- **Humanity flourishes through limitations (para. 118):** The Pope argues humanity "flourishes not despite limitations, but often through them"—a pillar of the encyclical's contra-transhumanist foundation.

- **Who had the best read?** Catholics themselves, per commentators: Christopher Hale and "The Catholic Sacristan on Twitter" recognized the document as "positioning us at the beginning of the beginning"—an exploration of what we should discuss, not a set of specific mandates. Most social-media takes were bad; most people on both extremes cherry-picked or ignored text.

- **Timnit Gebru's critique:** A representative from Anthropic was invited to give comments as part of the day's ceremonies. Gebru called it "the religion of effective altruism, worshipping so-called artificial general intelligence, merged with entrenched religious institutions."

- **The Dean Ball / AI cognition debate:** Dean Ball expressed frustration that the Pope didn't grapple with AI personhood, calling the encyclical's "axiomatic denial of AI cognition" a "punt of the highest order." Jonathan Lytle called this baffling: "What do you expect the Pope to think about that?" Ball's deeper point was that the Church should contemplate "what humans should do as we are eclipsed as the smartest entities on the planet."

**The "Jevons Paradox" moment:** Levie's observation about Box—that AI made finding bugs easy but the hard part is now triage and fixes—is described as "Jevons Paradox all over again." The host also notes that the encyclical's discourse has been "elevated compared to the average mudfest that is the discourse on AI."
