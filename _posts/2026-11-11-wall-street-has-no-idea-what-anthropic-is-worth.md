# Summary: Wall Street Has No Idea What Anthropic Is Worth

[AI Daily Brief - Wall Street Has No Idea What Anthropic Is Worth](https://podcasters.spotify.com/pod/show/nlw/episodes/Wall-Street-Has-No-Idea-What-Anthropic-Is-Worth-e3s7t73)

Anthropic has set its IPO price range at $70 to $78 per share, implying a valuation of roughly $1.25 to $1.4 trillion, and host Nathaniel Whittemore argues the entire valuation discourse -- from the splashy $2 trillion chatter to the final range -- proves that investors have no real idea what the company is worth until the stock actually trades. The episode also covers the deepening Nvidia China spy scandal, the official release of Google's Gemini 4 Carbon, and OpenAI's record revenue quarter. Anthropic is expected to begin trading on Nasdaq under the ticker "ANT" around November 19.

* **The IPO numbers**: Anthropic is offering 825 million Class A shares at $70-$78, seeking to raise $57-64 billion, plus a $26 billion private placement of Class N stock to industry partners including AMD, Broadcom, Google, Micron, Microsoft, Nvidia, and TSMC -- with Nvidia and Google in for up to $10 billion each. Combined with the greenshoe option, Anthropic will float around $100 billion in stock.
* **Anthropic never "settled" for less than $2 trillion**: The $2 trillion figure came from an anonymous investor source of unknown proximity to the company, and it stuck because it was splashy. Anthropic couldn't respond during its quiet period. The slide from $2T to $1.6T to $1.5T to talk of $1.1T says something about shifting investor mood, but it was never a goal Anthropic set and missed.
* **The range may already be too low**: Within a day of the $1.25-1.4 trillion range going public, the Financial Times reported Anthropic's bankers plan to raise the share price to reflect a top-end valuation of $1.5 trillion after "strong indications of demand." The FT added that Anthropic cares much more about raising cash than valuation, given its large compute commitments.
* **The contrarian case**: Artemis's John Ma notes the emerging consensus is bearish -- overvalued, revenue flattening, tokens commoditizing -- but the counter is that Anthropic's run-rate revenue rose a full $10 billion in October alone to $81 billion, still driven primarily by coding agents and reflecting Claude Opus 5.5 but not yet Fable 5.2. Finance, legal, and life sciences remain super early, and retail investors starving for direct frontier-lab exposure could push the stock toward $3 trillion regardless of valuation.
* **Priced like CrowdStrike, not Palantir**: iCapital's Sonali Basak notes that at $1.4 trillion, Anthropic trades at roughly 40x trailing 12-month revenue -- versus roughly 55x for Palantir -- putting it closer to the multiples of CrowdStrike and Cloudflare.
* **Compute commitments reshuffled**: Anthropic disclosed $10 billion in new seven-year commitments with Microsoft and Google, and said its $35 billion deal with neocloud Lambda is cancelable after Lambda defaulted on a deadline -- though it doesn't currently intend to cancel. This reframes the fear of hundreds of billions in non-cancellable compute commitments: which fails first, AI demand or the neoclouds' ability to finance and deliver data centers on time?
* **Claude Sheets, Whiteboards, and Data**: Alongside the IPO updates, Anthropic launched a productivity suite -- Claude Sheets (an Excel competitor built for human-agent collaboration), Claude Whiteboards (team planning), and Claude Data (evolved from Dashboards, competing with Power BI but adding model-generated data stories and narrated videos). Anthropic's own legal team cut marketing reviews from three days to 24 hours with a Claude-built Slack review tool. Microsoft fell 2.2% on the news, though the counterpoint is that Microsoft is investor, landlord (owed $45 billion in cloud spend), reseller via Copilot, and now competitor -- and Claude Sheets even exports to Excel.
* **Nvidia's spy scandal deepens**: The Information reports as much as $7.5 billion in Nvidia chips may have been funneled to Chinese companies through Megaspeed, the neocloud that employed alleged Chinese spy Alice Huang -- roughly 240,000 banned H100/H200 chips. The New York Times reports a Commerce Department investigation into Megaspeed was halted after Nvidia general counsel Tim Teter contacted investigators, and that Trump was fully briefed last April but did nothing. House Foreign Affairs Chair Brian Mast says he'll subpoena Jensen Huang if he won't appear, the Wall Street Journal reports Nvidia has no succession plan, and the stock is down 11% since the story broke -- with Nvidia making up about 7% of the S&P 500, the whole nation is experiencing "the mother of all keyman risks."
* **Gemini 4 Carbon officially ships**: Google previewed Carbon on October 28 as the #1 model on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index v5 but held it back for the government's voluntary safety testing; it became generally available November 10. DeepMind VP Pushmeet Kohli showcased Carbon formally verifying Signal's security properties -- work that "could take teams decades of combined effort" done in a month. On Pavel Huryn's Bug Hunt Bench (The Product Compass), Carbon found 57 of 105 real bugs for $31, one behind Fable 5.2's 58 at almost $140 -- near-parity at under a quarter of the cost.
* **OpenAI posts a record quarter**: Q3 revenue came in above $10 billion, up 50% from Q2, driven by API demand for GPT-6 Astra and GPT-5.6 Sol plus quietly successful image models; operating losses grew to $10.1-10.9 billion. OpenAI also acquired Odessia, the year-old AI travel agent founded by Sonder's Francis Davidson (which booked $60 million in travel last summer), and launched a business hub with analytics dashboards and "business agents" that consumers can chat with after clicking ads -- making ChatGPT an increasingly direct rival to Meta as a commerce platform.
* **A small Nvidia bright spot**: Government consulting giant Booz Allen Hamilton switched its cybersecurity and defense software from Anthropic's models to Nvidia's open-source Nemotron, reporting significant cost savings.

The through-line of the episode: every number thrown around about Anthropic's valuation -- $2 trillion, $1.1 trillion, $1.5 trillion -- came from anonymous sources and shifting sentiment rather than the company itself, and the market's 2.2% dip on a spreadsheet launch is just more evidence that nobody actually knows what this all means until the stock starts trading. The next phase of the AI trade begins next week either way.
