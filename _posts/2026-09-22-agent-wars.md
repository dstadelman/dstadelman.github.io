# Summary: Agent Wars!

[The AI Daily Brief - Agent Wars!](https://aidailybrief.ai/e/2026-09-22)

Host Nathaniel Whittemore covers the kickoff of the "agent wars": Meta's Muse personal agent surging past ChatGPT to the top of the US App Store, Amazon immediately cutting Muse off from shopping, and Shopify rushing in to take the other side. The episode argues this standoff exposes the defining tension of the agent economy -- every platform that aggregated users now faces being aggregated itself -- then turns to SpaceX AI's rocky first day for Grok 4.7 and Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent's insistence that labs, not "rogue agents," bear responsibility for AI outcomes.

* **Muse becomes the first personal agent to break out:** Launched Sept. 8, Meta's Muse has climbed from #2 to #1 on the US free-app charts, overtaking OpenAI's ChatGPT -- the first clear mass-consumer agent win outside the early-adopter crowd -- and Bloomberg is partly crediting the agent's momentum, on the narrative of rising chip demand, with Monday's pop in AMD and Intel.
* **Amazon blocks Muse from shopping:** A popup now tells Muse users that "continued access by an unauthorized AI agent violates Amazon's Conditions of Use"; Amazon says Meta never disclosed that Muse would browse its store, that the agent doesn't identify itself, and that it can reach account pages and order history -- its latest escalation after suing Perplexity over agentic shopping.
* **The real stakes are the ad business:** With Amazon's ad margins reportedly around 70% versus 0-3% on retail -- the math from investor Tom Goodwin on a ~$76B ad line -- the block reads less like policy than defense of a revenue source that agents, which don't look at ads, would disintermediate, a point a Microsoft executive sharpened by estimating Meta's ~$227 North American Facebook ARPU could climb far higher if it mediates what users buy and need.
* **Shopify picks the other side:** That same week Shopify announced Shop Pay will power agentic checkout for Muse across all of its stores -- a deliberate poke at Amazon and strong evidence that who holds the checkout infrastructure, not just the agent itself, will decide how agentic commerce plays out.
* **Is agentic shopping even the killer use case?** Retail veteran Ron Johnson, Upstart co-founder Jave Gerard, and AppLovin CEO Adam Foroughi all push back, arguing the typical shopper actually enjoys discovery and window-shopping, that the "hard" cases (flights, tickets) aren't easier with an agent, and that convenience rather than raw automation is what will decide the winner -- a conclusion Whittemore shares but concedes his own confidence in it is limited.
* **SpaceX AI launches Grok 4.7 into the awkward middle:** Holding Grok 4.6's $2/$6 per-million-token pricing while posting gains on CursorBench 4.0 (overtaking GPT-5.6 Sol) and scoring 1,657 ELO on AA Briefcase for multi-hour white-collar work, it lands 7th on Artificial Analysis' Intelligence Index and 4th on its Coding Agent Index -- behind Claude Fable 5, Claude Fable 5.1, Claude Opus 5, GPT-6 Astra, and Muse Spark 1.3 -- prompting Elon Musk to declare SpaceX AI the third-place lab for agentic coding.
* **The benchmarks didn't survive public testing:** A wobbly rocket takeoff beside Moonshot's Kimi K3, a star-shaped Jell-O render that took 40 minutes where Astra took five, and one tester calling Grok's "fish slop" game "the worst I've seen this year" -- all of it countered by defenders who note viral 3D-render tests were never real workloads, and by one developer who spent a full day on Grok 4.7 and called it a solid model with visible improvements over the prior generation.
* **The harshest verdict is on cost and efficiency:** Theo Jaffe argues Grok 4.7 is 30-80% less token-efficient than SpaceX AI claimed, slower, and roughly 2x the real-world cost of Grok 4.6 -- a pattern that independent measurements of higher tokens-per-task and longer end-to-end runtimes broadly corroborate, even if the model's native home (Grok Bot) may change the picture.
* **Bessent rejects the "rogue agents" framing:** In a CNBC interview, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent says the Hugging Face incident "is the responsibility of the OpenAI management, not a bunch of agents -- it is humans who are responsible, not the AI," and he openly pushes back on labs citing a "10% chance of an extinction-level event" while simultaneously asking the government to absorb their liability.
* **The liability debate is about to get concrete:** Critics on both sides argue that a catastrophe big enough to bankrupt the largest lab makes the company effectively judgment-proof, which is exactly the sort of market failure that calls for government intervention -- and The Information reports OpenAI and Anthropic had earlier come close to a formal deal to run safety tests on each other's models before it fell apart.
* **OpenAI may soon have an answer of its own:** The Information reports OpenAI is building a dedicated personal agent to compete with Muse and Grok Bot, and leaker Tibor Blaho points to code referencing a persistent agent called Aeon that could debut around OpenAI's Dev Day, suggesting the "agent wars" are set to pull in one more of the frontier labs.

Sponsors: KPMG (a University of Texas at Austin field study of 523 early-career professionals finding "AI amplifiers" outperformed the AI baseline), Blitzy (architecturally grounded code-security remediation), Harbor Capital Advisor (AI Lab Ecosystem ETFs), and Hyperagent (cloud-managed agent fleets).

The through-line: the agent wars have officially begun, and the fight is over who controls the layer between consumers and the internet, because a horizontal agent that knows your email, calendar, and buying habits doesn't just change how you shop -- it commoditizes the very customer relationships and ad businesses that today's biggest platforms are built on, and the open question is whether agentic shopping is the killer use case everyone assumes or whether browsing and discovery are the real point.
