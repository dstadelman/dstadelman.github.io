# Summary: Fable is Back -- Here's What You Should Try First

[The AI Daily Brief - Fable is Back: Here's What You Should Try First](https://podcasters.spotify.com/pod/show/nlw/episodes/Fable-is-Back-Heres-What-You-Should-Try-First-e3lhm3n)

After nearly three weeks under export controls, Anthropic's Claude Fable 5 model has been cleared by the U.S. Department of Commerce and is returning to global audiences starting July 1. The episode covers the broader AI landscape: OpenAI's inference cost optimization, Anthropic's new Sonnet 5 agentic model, Base44's proprietary coding model, AWS's billion-dollar FDE push, and SpaceX's community concessions around Memphis data centers.

* **Claude Fable 5 export controls lifted**: The Department of Commerce lifted restrictions on Claude Fable 5 and Claude Mythos 5 after Anthropic implemented new cybersecurity safeguards including a classifier trained to block the jailbreak behavior identified by Amazon. The model is now available globally across all paid subscriptions with a one-week period where usage counts toward the standard subscription limit (up to 50% of weekly usage) before credits become required. White House officials praised the government-private partnership, with Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick overseeing the resolution.
* **OpenAI halves inference costs on key models**: The Information reported that OpenAI engineers developed an optimization cutting inference requirements by roughly half for certain model configurations -- logged-out ChatGPT traffic can be served on just 100 GPUs. However, the technique was not disclosed and is being tested only on non-logged-in users, suggesting possible trade-offs in quality. Andrew Curran of Benchmark Ventures noted this may specifically target the "AI mom test" demographic -- less engaged users more tolerant of slight quality degradation. The breakthrough does not confirm a fundamental solution to the industry's compute crunch but any cost reduction for OpenAI's massive free user base could meaningfully impact profitability.
* **Claude Sonnet 5 launched as Anthropic's most agentic model**: Announced June 30 alongside Fable 5's return, Sonnet 5 is pitched as an agentic model that can plan and execute autonomously with tool use across browsers and terminals, at near-Opus-class performance for a fraction of the cost. At promotional pricing ($2M input / $10M output tokens through August) it undercuts Opus 4.8's rates. On GDPVal -- Ethan Mollick's benchmark for economically valuable AI work at UPenn -- Sonnet 5 slightly outperformed Opus 4.8, a result some analysts attribute to its agentic end-to-end execution capability rather than raw reasoning. Users caution that the model requires a fundamentally different operational approach -- it spawns sub-agents and stacks multiple PRs autonomously rather than responding conversationally like previous Sonnet versions.
* **Base44 launches proprietary Base 1 model**: The vibe-coding platform, backed by Wix, released its first in-house AI model fine-tuned on hundreds of millions of user interactions -- a play to control both costs and platform differentiation. CEO Maor Shlomo argued that narrowly trained models can compete with frontier generalists for focused workflows like web app building, echoing Cursor's strategy with Composer 2.5. The model also positions Base44 to pair its engineering harness with the proprietary model in tandem -- similar to approaches OpenAI and Anthropic have pursued with their own coding platforms.
* **AWS bets $1 billion on Forward Deployed Engineers**: AWS announced a new Frontier AI Engineering unit staffing thousands of FDEs who will embed inside customer organizations to help deploy AI systems, compressing timelines from months to days using agentic tools. VP Francessca Vasquez said the focus will be healthcare, government, and financial services -- industries with the strongest demand. The move follows similar pushes by OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, and Microsoft into dedicated FTE/FDE divisions, signaling that enterprise AI adoption has shifted from broad deployment to optimization of existing budgets.
* **SpaceX offers Memphis residents half-price Starlink**: Amid controversy over its Colossus data center near Memphis -- which operates 46 gas turbines off-grid without permits -- SpaceX announced the company will provide discounted half-price Starlink subscriptions and free hardware to Memphis-area residents. The move follows local complaints about air pollution and a lawsuit against the turbines, though the U.S. government recently intervened claiming Colossus as a national security matter. Several administration officials have been quoted pushing back on the optics of what some see as communities being "bought off" rather than genuinely shared in data center benefits.

The return of Claude Fable 5 marks the end of one of AI's most dramatic regulatory episodes -- but raises practical questions about how to use the model effectively during its subsidized availability window. The prevailing advice: save it for your hardest technical challenges, particularly strategic reasoning tasks where iterative pushback and genuine intellectual engagement matter more than raw speed. For routine coding, cheaper models like Sonnet 5 may offer better cost-performance tradeoffs -- though some users warn that Sonnet 5 requires a completely different mental model to use well.
