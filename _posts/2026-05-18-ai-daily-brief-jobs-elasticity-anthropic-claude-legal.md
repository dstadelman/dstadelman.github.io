## Episode Three: "In Defense of Tokenmaxxing"

The episode defends "tokenmaxxing" -- the practice of incentivizing employees to spend AI tokens -- against critics who call it wasteful. The author argues tokenmaxxing is essential for organizational exploration into the agentic era, where enterprises must experiment to figure out how to use agents effectively. The "capability overhang" -- the gap between what AI can do and what organizations get from it -- is widening as we shift from assisted AI (agents help me) to agentic AI (I set up conditions for agents to act). Without token experimentation, there is no way to discover what works for each enterprise in this paradigm shift.

## Episode Five: "The New Jobs AI Will Create"

The episode presents a framework for AI's job creation impact through demand elasticity. Rather than viewing AI as purely a labor supply story (more supply = cheaper labor = displacement), the author argues AI expands demand in six ways:

1. **Price elasticity** -- making things cheaper so new buyers enter
2. **Access elasticity** -- reducing provider scarcity and barriers
3. **Complexity elasticity** -- making opaque systems navigable (taxes, insurance, legal)
4. **Continuity elasticity** -- shifting from "occasional help" to "always-on monitoring"
5. **Personalization elasticity** -- bringing customization to the masses
6. **Relational elasticity** -- increasing the value of human provenance in goods and services

These translate into two unlock mechanisms: the **affordability unlock** (same services, lower cost activates long-tail demand) and the **possibility unlock** (entirely new services that were operationally impossible before, like continuous preventative healthcare).

The episode addresses the AGI objection -- "won't AGI just eat those jobs too?" -- with an argument that continuous demand expansion, specialization, and the relational value of human touch create persistent demand across many sectors even as AI capabilities grow.
