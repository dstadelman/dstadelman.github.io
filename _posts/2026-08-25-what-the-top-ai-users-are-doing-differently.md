# Summary: What the Top AI Users Are Doing Differently

[The AI Daily Brief - What the Top AI Users Are Doing Differently](https://podcasters.spotify.com/pod/show/nlw/episodes/What-the-Top-AI-Users-Are-Doing-Differently-e3nsrnp)

The August 25, 2026 episode of the AI Daily Brief digs into OpenAI's own data showing the gap between the most advanced enterprise AI users and the average has ballooned from 2.6x in January to 8.3x by the end of June -- and the host argues the entire difference is agents. Frontier firms have moved from chat-based assistance to delegation, and because agents take on increasingly valuable systems-level work, that shift compounds their lead over the rest. The episode pairs the research with the day's news: Meta's imminent "Hatch" consumer agent, NVIDIA's "central bank of compute" thesis, and a peek at what Microsoft employees are quietly burning on AI.

* **The gap is agents, not models**: OpenAI's Enterprise Signals research defines frontier firms as the top 10% of usage (output tokens per active user) and typical firms as the 45th-55th percentile; that gap ran about 2x through 2025, widened to 2.6x in January, then exploded to 8.3x by June ([source](https://openai.com/signals/enterprise-data/)).
* **Token volume is diverging, not seat count**: over the same ~18-month window, the average firm is using roughly 2x the tokens it was a year and a half ago, while frontier firms are up 17x -- they are not just using agents more, but better.
* **The agentic "flippening"**: in August 2025 (at GPT-5's launch) enterprise output tokens were essentially 100% ChatGPT; by February the split was 87% chat / 13% agentic, 73/27 by March, crossed 53% agentic by late April (GPT-5.5 launch), and hit 64% agentic by June.
* **Non-technical roles are growing fastest**: in the a16z-reposted "Codex usage by job title" chart (indexed from February), engineering grew 5x while finance/accounting rose 20x, marketing/communications 26x, people/recruiting and sales each 41x, and legal 108x.
* **Two compounding drivers**: per OpenAI, (1) more work is being delegated to agents, and (2) that delegation compounds -- the firms farthest along get farther ahead because agents take on increasingly valuable work.
* **Skills and plugins separate the leaders**: typical firms report ~9% of weekly active users on plugins and ~3% on skills; frontier firms are at ~19% skills and ~21% plugins; at OpenAI itself, 93% of employees use skills and 95% use plugins.
* **A "use-case ladder"**: the host frames the shift from chat to agentic as climbing rungs -- generation (drafting a report or formula), synthesis (combining disparate sources), execution (an agent interacting with real systems), and maintenance (an agent owning a system over time).
* **Legal as the case study**: in chat, legal work is 57% writing and ~20.5% knowledge retrieval with system operation barely registering (0.2%); in agentic use, writing drops to ~16% while classification/extraction jumps to ~4.6%, system operations to ~17.7%, workflow automation to ~7.7%, and even coding rises to ~33%.
* **It's a workflow problem, not a model problem**: OpenAI's own framing is that the gains come from how firms put models to work -- moving from assistance to delegation, giving agents context and tools, and accelerating agentic AI beyond software -- and the host adds that knowledge work's slower progress is being unlocked by RL, scaling, and evals like GDPVAL.
* **KPMG's "AI Amplifiers"**: a joint KPMG + University of Texas at Austin field study of 523 early-career professionals found the best performers are not defined by what they know, but by how they work with AI -- guiding, evaluating, and refining its outputs -- with roughly half of workers landing in that top "AI Amplifier" profile ([source](https://hrtechedition.com/news/kpmg-mccombs-ai-skills-gap-behavioral-study/)).
* **Mind-muscle memory is the real barrier**: the growth in agentic knowledge work came from figuring out patterns that let agents thrive in real contexts, not just from better models, and the host argues the next gains will come from "multiplayer" and team-level AI rather than individual agent silos.
* **Sam Altman has reversed on the timeline**: in a recent podcast interview, Altman says he was wrong about the speed -- "the economy just has so much inertia" -- and admits even he keeps doing rote computer tasks the old way by "revealed preference" despite knowing better; the host reads this as evidence the transition will be slower and smoother than the jobs-apocalypse framing.
* **Meta's "Hatch" is weeks away**: per roadmap documents seen by The Information, Meta is finishing a consumer agent (internally "Hatch") -- a streamlined, OpenClaw-style experience -- that could anchor a new AI-agent subscription with a ~$200/month tier for heavy users, with a possible small-group preview as soon as this week.
* **WhatsApp becomes a multi-agent platform**: Meta reportedly plans a WhatsApp platform for third-party agents to coordinate via messages, which the host says mirrors Grok Bot more than it is "cribbing" it -- these are just emerging interaction patterns.
* **Meta's "Watermelon" targets October**: back in July, Meta's Alexandr Wang told staff the larger model had caught GPT-5.5 on internal benchmarks, but the frontier has since moved to GPT-5.6, leaving open whether Watermelon can keep pace.
* **Grok Bot pricing finally settled**: the confusing "pay for both Cursor Ultra and SuperGrok Heavy (~$500/mo)?" launch has simplified -- Grok Bot is now included in the $60/month Cursor Pro plan and the $100/month SuperGrok plan ([source](https://x.ai/news/grok-bot-more-plans)).
* **OpenAI cuts GPT-5.6 Sol pricing**: API access drops to $4/M input and $20/M output (from $5/$30), following earlier cuts on Luna and Terra -- a move the host reads as OpenAI courting cost-conscious business customers rather than merely pressuring Anthropic ahead of its IPO ([source](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/models/gpt-5.6-sol)).
* **Hugging Face in talks at $13B**: Business Insider reports HF is courting an acquisition at a ~$13B valuation, justified in part by $150M+ in annualized revenue (up 50% in two months) despite 97% of users on free; CEO Clement Delangue said premium accounts doubled in H1 and the platform is approaching profitability ([source](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/24/hugging-face-reportedly-in-talks-to-be-acquired-for-13b/)).
* **NVIDIA as "central bank of compute"**: a week of dealmaking (a Poolside license + talent buy, a stake in Mercor, a possible Perplexity investment near $30B, plus neocloud and power deals) has led The Information's Martin Peers to liken Jensen's strategy to John Malone's 1980s cable empire; NVIDIA reported $42.3B invested in private companies as of its March earnings call.
* **The chip-smuggling angle**: Taiwanese prosecutors have charged nine people, including an Nvidia distribution manager and two SuperMicro staff, for smuggling up to 130 Blackwell B300 servers to China (74 delivered, 56 stopped) -- less than 10,000 chips, real but not a frontier-cluster threat.
* **Microsoft's AI bills, self-reported**: Business Insider obtained an internal spreadsheet where ~350 of ~600 reporting US staff log salary and AI spend; BI found no correlation between token burn and comp/promotions, with median monthly AI spend clustering around $150-500 but outliers reaching $7,500 (Azure), $15,000 (Cloud & AI), and one bill of $28,000 (Customer & Partner Solutions).

The frontier of AI use is no longer about access to a smart model -- it is about whether a team has moved from asking an assistant questions to delegating real, verifiable, systems-level work to agents, and once that shift starts it compounds, which is exactly why the gap between the top decile and everyone else has blown past 8x in six months.
