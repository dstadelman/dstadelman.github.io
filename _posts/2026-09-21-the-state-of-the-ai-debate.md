# Summary: The State of the AI Debate

[The AI Daily Brief - The State of the AI Debate](https://aidailybrief.ai/e/2026-09-21)

This episode of the AI Daily Brief covers the next phase of the AI policy debate -- Trump's push for a new "AI Force" and a rebrand of the term, governors championing kill switches and data-center restrictions, and Anthropic's IPO slipping to November under a safety-shaped cloud. The thread tying it all together is China: with President Xi Jinping arriving in Washington for a Sept. 23-25 visit and fresh US-China AI talks just opened, every US policy move is now backstopped by assumptions about what Beijing will actually agree to. The show's core claim is that the two AI superpowers aren't even worried about the same things -- America debates existential risk while its AI buildout props up the economy, while China worries about espionage and party control while getting little economic lift from AI at all.

* **Trump forms an "AI Force" and tries to rebrand the tech:** In a Truth Social post on Saturday, Trump said he's forming an "AI Force" much like the Space Force and will soon name an "AI czar" ("Only High I.Q. individuals need apply"). In a follow-up poll on renaming the field, "Superior Intelligence" edged out "Extreme Intelligence" 51-49 after Trump dropped "Supreme Intelligence" over its Supreme Court association, and he claimed AI could reach as much as 25% of US GDP.
* **Governors embrace kill switches and data-center rules:** California Gov. Gavin Newsom signed an executive order to accelerate an AI-kill-switch policy and add third-party safety inspectors, while Virginia Gov. Abigail Spanberger signed Executive Order 22 and a Data Center Accountability Framework that bans non-disclosure agreements, ends by-right approval, and tightens noise and backup-power rules in a state that hosts the world's densest data-center cluster (Loudoun County).
* **Anthropic punts its IPO to November:** The Wall Street Journal reported Anthropic won't list this month or next as planned; advisers say the decision was made before CEO Dario Amodei's call for an AI slowdown and reflects a desire to show a full third-quarter print, even as some on Wall Street begin treating AI-safety risk as a market risk.
* **Anthropic's profits come with a big asterisk:** Testing-the-waters leaks say Anthropic went from spending $2.30 per dollar of revenue a year ago to slightly profitable, with gross margins above 80% only after stripping out stock-based comp, revenue-sharing deals, and model-training costs -- a framing critics argued paper profitability isn't a strong sign of financial health.
* **Anthropic quietly stands up a biology "wet lab":** Reuters reported Anthropic established a Bay Area wet lab for physical biology work; head of life sciences Eric Kauderer-Abrams said real lab work remains biology's "final test," the focus is fundamental biology rather than drug discovery, and Novo Nordisk is a partner -- drawing barbed jokes from investors like Chamath Palihapitiya.
* **First cracks in data-center debt:** Bonds tied to a Jane Street-leased data center, issued in August at 8.9%, now trade near 11.3%; Meta's first junk-bond tap via CleanSpark raised roughly $2.3 billion at 8.25% on about $10 billion of demand; and $18 billion of Oracle debt tied to a New Mexico data center is quoted as low as 89 cents on the dollar -- signposts the credit cycle, rather than stock prices, is where to watch for an AI-trade rollover.
* **Bessent opens a US-China AI dialogue:** Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent and Chinese Vice Premier He Lifeng held talks in New York, agreeing to a US-China AI dialogue and an incident-notification line ahead of the Sept. 24 Trump-Xi summit; separately, Rep. Ro Khanna convened an emergency congressional hearing and sent letters to Chinese labs (Alibaba, DeepSeek, and Moonshot) urging a binding agreement to pace the frontier.
* **China's priorities are different:** Beijing's AI-safety stance centers on party control and data sovereignty -- echoed by state-security official Chen Yixin and a state-media warning that Anthropic shares data with US intelligence -- rather than existential risk; meanwhile the New York Times reports Chinese economists see no AI-driven recovery, with youth unemployment at 18.9%.

The episode's through-line is that US AI policy has effectively become a two-country conversation: kill switches, pauses, and pacing the frontier all hinge on whether China will play along this week, and the two governments appear to be solving different problems entirely -- America's fear of an existential race and a booming buildout, versus China's concern over espionage, party control, and a boom that isn't lifting an economy struggling with high youth joblessness and soft consumer demand.
