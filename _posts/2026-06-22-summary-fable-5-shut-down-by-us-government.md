# Summary: Fable 5 Shut Down by US Government

[AI Daily Brief - Fable 5 Shut Down by US Government](https://podcasters.spotify.com/pod/show/nlw/episodes/Fable-5-Shut-Down-by-US-Government-e3koa10)

On June 12, 2026, the U.S. Commerce Department issued an export control directive ordering Anthropic to suspend all access to its most advanced AI models -- Fable 5 and Mythos 5 -- for any foreign national, whether inside or outside the United States. The order caught the AI industry by complete surprise when announced via late-night tweet, abruptly cutting off hundreds of millions of users worldwide and triggering widespread criticism from policymakers, industry leaders, and international observers. According to a Wall Street Journal report, the directive was triggered by jailbreak research conducted at Amazon, which identified prompts that could extract information about security vulnerabilities from Anthropic's models. Anthropic disputed the government's justification, noting that the alleged jailbreak technique is broadly available across other models and does not constitute a universal compromise -- but was forced to comply or face potential penalties.

* **The export control directive**: Commerce Department Secretary Howard Lutnick sent a letter to Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei (confirmed spelling) ordering suspension of Fable 5 and Mythos 5 for all foreign nationals globally. Anthropic complied immediately, disabling access across the board rather than attempting selective enforcement.

* **The reported trigger**: The WSJ later reported that researchers at Amazon used prompts to get Fable 5 to reveal information about cybersecurity vulnerabilities. Amazon reportedly shared these findings with the U.S. government, though the WSJ did not definitively confirm Amazon was the source. Anthropic stated the government only provided verbal evidence of a narrow, non-universal jailbreak -- essentially prompting the model to read a codebase and fix software flaws -- which is capability widely available in other models including OpenAI's GPT-5.5 used daily by cybersecurity defenders.

* **Anthropic's public response**: In its blog post, Anthropic argued it had worked with the U.S. government extensively on red-teaming Fable's safeguards before release and that no universal jailbreak has been found. The company pointed out that every industry safeguard is vulnerable to narrow, non-universal jailbreaks, and that the narrow technique reported to the government required a 30-day customer data retention policy -- a real cost the company absorbed. Anthropic maintained that even if a potential jailbreak exists, it should not warrant recalling a commercial model deployed to hundreds of millions.

* **EB-1 visa implications**: A huge number of Anthropic's technical staff, including Andrej Karpathy (confirmed spelling), are not U.S. citizens but hold EB-1 extraordinary ability visas. This means they themselves are now prohibited from interacting with the company's flagship models, making the restriction internally as well as externally disruptive.

* **Government hypocrisy calls**: Multiple critics highlighted the apparent contradiction in an administration that pushes to export advanced AI chips to China while simultaneously restricting global access to the best U.S. AI models. Chris McGuire, CFR Senior Fellow, wrote the Commerce Department's export strategy is "completely incoherent and self-defeating," and multiple commentators pointed to White House OSTP tweets from weeks earlier criticizing government oversight of new models as "chilling effects on free speech and innovation."

* **Industry scorn on Anthropic**: While the government faced criticism, many industry voices criticized Anthropic for how it handled the situation. Commenters accused CEO Dario Amodei of scaremongering that first spooked politicians, then backfired when his own company was the target. Multiple critics wrote that Anthropic's public posture of acting as the sole arbiter of AI safety appeared arrogant, especially given the company has publicly sought regulatory capture for the IPO. A viral three-panel cartoon depicted Amodei warning Trump about dangers, Trump ordering a ban, and then a furious Amodei saying "you can't do this."

* **Global sovereign AI implications**: The directive has energized sovereign AI efforts worldwide. Commentators noted that a defensible argument now exists for EU model preference, British and European hedging toward Chinese open-weight alternatives, and that nation-states will soon need citizenship or security clearance requirements for next-generation models similar to nuclear technology controls. British MP Tom Tugendhat (confirmed spelling) warned Britain cannot continue tying itself to the past while emphasizing safety over opportunity.

* **Market and IPO impact**: The event has sent shockwaves through AI markets and investor sentiment. Multiple observers noted this effectively kneecaps Anthropic's path to a trillion-dollar IPO by removing 25% of its global market share. Analysts questioned whether the intelligence levels of competing models (Opus 4.7 and GPT-5.5) justify the massive AI capital expenditure being deployed, and noted that OpenAI and Google DeepMind now face reduced incentives to ship comparable models lest they trigger their own export controls.

**The primary takeaway**: This event marks a defining turning point in AI regulation -- the first time the U.S. government has directly barred foreign nationals from using commercially available frontier AI models. It creates precedent for future government intervention in AI development, demonstrates the vulnerability of companies that have cultivated adversarial government relationships, and may accelerate a global split in AI model access that benefits non-U.S. AI competitors and sovereign AI initiatives worldwide.
