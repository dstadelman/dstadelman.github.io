# Summary: Fable 5.2 Is the Best AI Ever. So Why Am I Not Switching Yet?

[AI Daily Brief - Fable 5.2 Is the Best AI Ever. So Why Am I Not Switching Yet?](https://podcasters.spotify.com/pod/show/nlw/episodes/Fable-5-2-Is-the-Best-AI-Ever--So-Why-Am-I-Not-Switching-Yet-e3s60fp)

With Anthropic's IPO approaching, the company released Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2, and the first reactions capture a paradox: it is easy to call the most powerful model ever built, yet hard to say when most people should actually reach for it over Opus 5.5. Host Nathaniel Whittemore rips through the benchmarks, first impressions, and deep-dive analyses to place the model in real stacks. The conclusion: Fable 5.2 is opportunity AI, not efficiency AI -- a spiky model that pays off when you hand it whole projects and attempt things that were not possible before.

* **Benchmarks lift the bar across the board:** On Frontier Code, Fable 5.2 is four points ahead of GPT-6 Astra and 3.5 ahead of Opus 5.5; on Terminal-Bench Science it is six points ahead of GPT-6 Astra; and on cybersecurity, Mythos 5.2 beats the previous leader by about 10 points in each case.
* **New emerging-capability benchmarks:** Anthropic added non-standard scorecards -- Fable 5.2 scored 64.9% on RoboDojo Sim, 25 points above Opus 5.5's already-breakout result, and Mythos 5.2 hit 85.1% on protein design, six points above Mythos 5.1.
* **The one complicated result:** On agentic knowledge work, Fable 5.2 completed about five points more tasks than Fable 5.1, Opus 5.5, and Gemini 4 Carbon, but its ELO score was lower than Opus 5.5's -- it finishes more work without necessarily doing it better than its smaller stablemate.
* **The most alarming score:** On the Remote Labor Index of real-world contractor tasks, Fable 5.2 scored 41.3%, nearly doubling Opus 5.5's 21% (the best agent a year ago managed 2.5%). Obsolete author Garrison Lovely called it by far the most alarming score -- still a leading indicator, but a reminder of how fast change comes under exponential growth.
* **New #1 on Artificial Analysis:** Fable 5.2's index score of 65 puts it five points ahead of Gemini 4 Carbon, six ahead of Opus 5.5, and eight ahead of GPT-6 Astra, and its extra-high (62) and high (62) settings fill the next two spots. Gains came mostly from science, knowledge, and coding, while agentic knowledge work and terminal coding sometimes only reached Opus 5.5 levels.
* **Cheaper per token, still very expensive per task:** Per-token prices dropped 20% to $8 input / $40 output per million, but at max effort Fable costs $26.54 per task across the AA suite -- more than double Fable 5.1, 2.6x Opus 5.5, and over 4x GPT-6 Astra. Extra high drops that to $14.83 and high to $9.08, 10% cheaper than Opus 5.5 on max, and early tester Matt Schumer argues even low effort covers most tasks.
* **Anthropic's own effort guidance:** Medium should be the default, high is enough for everyday coding, extra high is for long-running agentic work with measurable outcomes, and max "is for finding the ceiling and not recommended for everyday use."
* **Anthropic reserves Fable for jobs other models can't do:** Claude now writes 90% of Anthropic's code changes under human supervision, with Fable 5.2 brought in for stubborn bugs, performance work, and high-value projects -- such as migrating a critical production service from Python to Rust.
* **An operating system from an empty folder:** Fable 5.2 coordinated over 1,000 sub-agents working around the clock for eight days to produce a working OS that boots on a laptop, with its own core, drivers for disk/keyboard/network, permanent file storage, windows, a login screen, an installer, and built-in Claude functionality -- prompting AI entrepreneur Theo's now-famous "Yeah, and?" reaction.
* **A deliberate science push:** Fable 5.2 solved two open theoretical-physics problems about black holes, accurately modeled 28 of 34 gene-therapy capsids, and fully formalized Fermat's Last Theorem into a machine-readable proof in 30 hours with 1,000 agents -- where Opus 5.5 stalled 26% of the way through after 18 hours.
* **Robotics and the bitter lesson:** In Anthropic's demo, Fable 5.2 piloted a pair of robotic arms, outperforming most models specifically designed to drive them, even on tasks it was never explicitly trained to do.
* **Security is a standout:** Fitting Mythos's origin story, testers found the commercial model extremely strong on security -- one found 10+ severe vulnerabilities created earlier by Opus 5.5 without even being asked. On AI entrepreneur Pavel Hurin's bug hunt bench (105 planted bugs in real repos), Fable 5.2 found 58 versus 40-45 for most top models and 51 for Sonnet 5.5 Max -- with less than half of Sonnet's turns for less money.
* **First impressions from testers:** 3D AI engineer Stefan Vaskevich re-ran his September Opus 5.5 Blender castle prompt and Fable 5.2 produced radically more detail; Riley Brown generated a full 60-second ad live on stream in a single shot, drawing a "Whoa" from Claude Code creator Boris Cherny; and Allie K. Miller's ambitious early-test list (a water-safety map of 3,100 US cities, an ADHD-themed YouTube channel, psychological profiles of her stalkers, a 3D hot dog kiosk to-do manager) ended with "I trusted it more than any other model I've tested."
* **Every's verdict -- a paradigm shift if you know what to do with it:** A teammate built a working video editor combining Premiere's editing, After Effects titles, and Ableton-style mixing with an AI agent running all of it. Their framing: Opus 5.5 keeps you in the loop, but Fable 5.2 "can take the whole product" -- the first model that lets professionals build production-grade tools without ever reading the code.
* **Spiky, jagged, and honest about it:** Every's Dan Shipper called it "a spiky kind of nuts" -- some tasks blow your mind like nothing before, everything else is sort of normal and Opus 5.5 is the better choice.
* **Ethan Mollick's "Jagged AGI":** Mollick frames the model as almost AGI, but its failures changed character -- an accurate but joyless course, a 182-page paper, creative work buried under rules. Its critics and fact-checkers caught errors but never asked whether the work was too long, repetitive, or no fun: "Knowing when your own work is not good enough is a large part of what makes someone an expert, and it's the part Fable does least reliably."
* **Fable doesn't know what humans find impressive:** Whittemore re-ran Mollick's "show off your capabilities" prompt -- in the regular Claude interface Fable made things hard to make but easy to check (a self-counting sentence, music that harmonizes backwards, a hand-built QR code) that felt underwhelming; in Claude Code it produced a far more interesting three-minute film computed frame-by-frame in the browser. Both outputs, Mollick's and NLW's, ended with the same hidden acrostic twist.
* **Its first episode pitch was unfathomably boring:** Asked for an impressive data-driven AI Daily Brief idea, Fable proposed timing every AI launch from announcement to availability -- a real gap in coverage that nobody cares about. Its backup idea, grading every dated AI prediction that has come due, was genuinely interesting, though NLW thinks Opus 5.5 would do roughly as well.
* **Games may be the next medium:** Anthropic's Sholto Douglas called Fable 5.2 the first model that passes his bar for "seriously fun and beautiful games" -- real game mechanics, not good-looking shells -- and Matt Schumer built a playable Harry Potter-themed battle royale. NLW's question: instead of making a deck or a website, should you make a game?
* **The most common critique:** For most uses Fable is not better than Opus 5.5, just more annoying -- stubborn, guardrail-heavy, poor taste, expensive, and token-hungry. Some think that's exactly why this release is 5.2 rather than 5.5: no Opus-5-to-5.5-style revolutionary leap.
* **Tips for using it well:** Anthropic's Alex Albert says to ask what's wrong with your idea before it builds anything, set approval points up front and make clear the rest is its call, start on medium or high effort, and most importantly give it a whole project instead of a next step and let it run for hours. Anthropic's updated prompting guide adds: tell it that it owns the codebase, and it will merge duplicate code and make related fixes without being asked. Daisy Hollman of Claude Code advises Opus 5.5 for tasks where you know what you want, Fable 5.2 for creative tasks when you don't.

The primary takeaway: Fable 5.2 is opportunity AI, not efficiency AI -- a model that asks you to push toward things that weren't possible before (complete software, live agentic video, playable games) rather than to improve what you already do. But it is a spiky model whose weaknesses are subtle failures of taste and self-judgment, and at this capability level, figuring out what a new release is actually for takes weeks of experimentation, not hours -- which is why NLW isn't switching his everyday stack yet.
