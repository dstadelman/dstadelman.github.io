# Summary: Companies Are Spending 5x More on AI Now

[AI Daily Brief - Companies Are Spending 5x More on AI Now ](https://podcasters.spotify.com/pod/show/nlw/episodes/Companies-Are-Spending-5x-More-on-AI-Now-e3rteiq)

In this November 6, 2026 Headlines-plus-main-episode edition, host Nathaniel Whittemore (NLW) digs into Menlo Ventures' fourth annual State of Enterprise AI report, which shows business AI spending not slowing but accelerating -- nearly $177 billion in 2026, up 5.4x from last year, with agents as the driver. The headlines cover the brutal market selloff that triggered a hedge fund deleveraging cascade, wobbly odds for Anthropic's planned IPO, and METR's first independent safety assessment of Anthropic, which found AI-driven R&D has roughly doubled the lab's pace of progress. The through-line: the mainstream narrative of companies reining in AI spend is backwards, because budgets built for seats are colliding with a per-token reality.

* **Market rout, worst day since 2020**: AI stocks had their worst drawdown since 2020, with the Mag 7 losing a combined trillion dollars in market cap -- Nvidia fell 9.3%, Microsoft 3.6%, Micron 17.5%, and SanDisk 22.3%; the S&P 500 dropped 4.4% and the Nasdaq 6.4%, and the selling was strikingly broad-based, hitting even Walmart and ExxonMobil.
* **No news catalyst, so look at market structure**: nothing substantial changed between Wednesday and Thursday, so the driver appears to be forced deleveraging -- Fundstrat's Tom Lee told CNBC there are stories of large hedge funds being liquidated, $17B Walleye Capital restructured its stock-picking business and is returning capital, Lone Pine is rumored to have collapsed (reporting so far documents a severe drawdown, not a confirmed closure), and Millennium reportedly pulled $1.5 billion in leveraged capital from two outside managers.
* **Forced selling everywhere at once**: Bloomberg's Nishant Kumar said he couldn't remember a day like it in 16 years covering hedge funds, and Nicholas Mugali of TMT Research wrote that the best businesses were falling hardest because they're the easiest things to sell when funds need cash.
* **Anthropic IPO wobbling**: with the lab aiming to list before Thanksgiving and raise up to $100 billion, prediction-market odds of an IPO before Thanksgiving fell from about 85% to 60%, and the Wall Street Journal reported three major IPO investors say the expected ~$1.5 trillion valuation needs to come down -- one arguing closer to $1 trillion -- though the same article reported an $80 billion revenue run rate for October, up from $65 billion at end of July.
* **METR's independent Anthropic assessment**: after an 11-week high-access evaluation, METR concluded Anthropic is improving model capabilities at roughly twice its March 2024-October 2025 rate (1.9x on its internal benchmark index), confidently ruled out training-compute scaling as the cause, and attributed about 80% of the acceleration to AI doing the R&D itself -- with Anthropic redacting most of the most informative evidence, drawing criticism from co-founder Sam McCandlish's own acknowledgment that a supplemental Anthropic analysis is coming.
* **Safety community rattled**: METR's Ryan Greenblatt called it "a big update towards ASI within one to two years," Encode's Nathan Calvin called it "extremely unnerving," New York Assemblyman Alex Bores argued Congress should ban recursive self-improvement this year, while researcher Nathan Lambert and others pushed back on the methodology -- and critics noted the report is essentially non-responsive to cybersecurity concerns, assuming RSI is the only risk that matters.
* **Claude gets personal finance**: Anthropic relaunched Claude for personal finance with read-only bank account connections via Plaid -- budgeting and spending analysis only, no investment advice and no ability to move money, which softens the concern from big banks about agents making transactions.
* **Muse now has a voice**: Meta rolled out voice and video calls with its Muse personal agent, powered by Muse Realtime Voice and Muse Realtime Avatar models that animate user-chosen avatars with expressions matched to conversation tone (though without visual input); chief AI officer Alexandr Wang demoed his fluffy-dog agent coaching him about posting too many memes, and the same tech will power the forthcoming Muse Charm wearable.
* **OpenAI Images 2.6 adds GIFs**: the new image model supports animated GIFs with editable frames and speed, is 50% faster with generation in as little as six seconds, accepts reference images like rough doodles, and accompanies OpenAI's claim that users now create 4 billion images per week, up from 3 billion in September.
* **The main stat -- enterprise AI spend hit ~$177B**: Menlo Ventures' estimates went from $1.7B in 2023 to $8.5B in 2024 to $33B in 2025 to almost $177B in 2026, and the growth rate sped up (5.4x this year vs 5.0x then 3.9x) even from a much bigger base -- the explanation being agents, since spend stopped being about seats and started being about tokens.
* **Breadth flat, depth exploding**: the share of enterprises using AI rose only from 55% to 65% (35% still use none), but average spend per enterprise jumped 4.7x -- and companies spent about 2.5x what they had budgeted, having planned for roughly 1.9x growth under a per-seat model that no longer describes reality.
* **Buyers are paying for capability, not savings**: frontier models' share of spend went from 35% to 63% even as labs cut small-model prices 80%+ -- today's popular models, Claude Opus 5.5 ($4/$20 per million tokens) and GPT-6 Astra ($10/$60), cost more than last year's leaders Claude Sonnet 4.5 ($3/$15) and GPT-5 ($1.25/$10).
* **A two-horse race**: Anthropic's share of enterprise API spend crested over half at 55% (from 40%), OpenAI recovered to 31% (from 27%) on the strength of the GPT-5.6 Sol/Terra/Luna releases in July and GPT-6 in September, while Google fell from 21% to 9%; near-frontier models like Grok 4.7, Kimi K3, DeepSeek V4, and GLM-5.3 together still hold under 5%.
* **Open source: cheap tokens, small dollars**: open-source models take just 4% of spend (down from 6%) but now handle about 19% of tokens (up from 11%), with Kimi dominant at 61% of open-source spending -- NLW argues token share is the more meaningful long-term signal.
* **Lab coding agents are the breakout**: Claude Code and Codex revenue jumped from $1 billion at end of 2025 to $24 billion now -- 24x in a single year -- though third-party coding platforms (Cursor, Cognition, Factory, Lovable, etc.) also nearly tripled from $2.6B to $7.8B; coding apps overall hit $34B, and financial services apps grew fastest in rate (8x) but on a tiny $800M base.
* **AI-natives vs incumbents split by domain**: AI-native companies take 96% of coding spend, 95% of finance/operations, and 81% of design, but incumbents keep more than half of marketing, customer success, HR, data science, and IT spend -- in IT, AI-natives get only 9%, because enterprises extend the systems of record they already run.
* **SaaS apocalypse didn't happen (yet)**: SaaS application market growth dipped only from 13% to 11%, and 55% of AI dollars came from net new budgets versus about 21% from existing software budgets -- though NLW flags the next threat to SaaS: agents using software directly and headless interfaces.

The primary takeaway: while headlines focus on companies like Apple capping employee AI spending, Menlo's survey of ~400 enterprise decision-makers shows the opposite at the aggregate level -- agents turned AI from a per-seat purchase into a per-token bill that scales with work performed, so spending is accelerating from an ever-bigger base and blowing past budgets by 2.5x. The episode's closing caution applies to the whole show: the narratives circulating in heavily-engaged AI circles still run ahead of the lived reality of the average business buying AI today.
