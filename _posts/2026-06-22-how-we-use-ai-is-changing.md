# How the AI Industry Is Changing: From Chatbots to Agents

## The AI Sovereign Wealth Fund Debate Goes Mainstream

The most striking development this week involves the possibility of the U.S. government taking equity stakes in major AI companies. This isn't just one side of the aisle -- both Bernie Sanders and Donald Trump have independently floated similar proposals.

Trump confirmed recent reports that his administration is exploring government equity stakes in AI labs, saying "there's a concept out there where pieces could be given to the American public" so "the American people can benefit from the success of AI." This aligns with longstanding talk of a sovereign wealth fund early in his term, and follows the government's recent precedent of taking stakes in companies including Intel.

Sam Altman has met with Bernie Sanders to discuss the concept of OpenAI donating equity to seed a public wealth fund, potentially through dividend distributions to citizens or "Trump Accounts for Children."

David Sacks, the Trump adviser and former AISR, offered a nuanced perspective: while he doesn't support socialism, he "can see why Bernie Sanders' proposal for the government to take a 50% stake in AI companies resonates" with many on the right. But he warned that "nationalization of AI will accelerate the corporate-government fusion we're already sliding towards," arguing that conservatives should be "even more concerned about central government AI" than about central bank digital currency.

Investor Brad Gertzner argued the mechanism matters most: "I don't trust shares in the hands of some future politicians that can coerce or liquidate and spend on whatever their political beliefs. If purchased or donated, the shares should be directly held by American citizens."

## Google Signs Massive $920M/Month Compute Deal with SpaceX

Google signed a three-year deal with SpaceX to rent compute through the Colossus supercluster. Google will pay $920 million monthly for at least 110,000 NVIDIA GPUs, running from October 2026 through June 2029. The deal includes early termination clauses on 90 days' notice and a right to exit in October if SpaceX fails to deliver full capacity.

SpaceX is completing its IPO at $150 per share later this week. Google owns a 6% stake valued at potentially $100 billion. Short seller Jim Chanos drew parallels to the dot-com bubble, while others calculated that with $26 billion/year in licensing to xAI, the data center payback period would be approximately 18 months.

## NVIDIA Secures Next-Gen Memory Supply

NVIDIA and SK Hynix announced a multi-year agreement securing high-bandwidth memory (HBM) supply through NVIDIA's transition to its Vera Rubin chips. The deal deepens NVIDIA's partnership with its largest memory supplier and includes NVIDIA joining as a design partner on new memory chips for physical AI and personal AI.

Jensen Huang traveled to Seoul for the announcement, continuing his pattern of hands-on deal-making with SK Group Chairman Lee Jay-yun.

## OpenAI's ChatGPT Overhaul -- A Shift in How We Use AI

The main story is OpenAI's planned transformation of ChatGPT into a "super app" combining coding tools and AI agents. The Financial Times reported the overhaul will begin rolling out in the coming weeks, with changes to the website and mobile apps encouraging more use of coding, image generation, and external partner apps.

The business case is clear: OpenAI has nearly a billion weekly ChatGPT users but loses $14 billion annually. Enterprise revenue now reaches about 40% of total revenue (2 million businesses), with a target of 50% by December.

CFO Sarah Friar noted power users are "burning through subscriptions so fast that they're just getting more subscriptions" -- a sign that heavy users get exponentially more value than casual ones.

The real story, I think, is less about money and more about a fundamental shift in AI usage patterns:

**Power users are using AI differently -- and getting more out of it.** KPMG and the University of Texas at Austin analyzed 1.4 million real workplace AI interactions and found that the highest-impact users treat AI "like a reasoning partner" -- they frame problems, guide thinking, iterate, and push for better answers.

**The advantage gap is widening dramatically.** What used to be a modest differential between power user and casual user value has exploded. When coding agents became viable late last year, the gap shifted into overdrive. People using agents see compounding value; those using regular chat see only linear gains.

**Agents are the new interface.** This is what the ChatGPT super app really means. The most productive interactions aren't chats -- they're loops. Peter Steinberger (OpenClaw creator, now OpenAI) noted that power users increasingly run "loops" that automate repeated AI tasks. Boris Cherny, Anthropic's coding agent creator, told CNBC that his own workflow has evolved from "prompting Claude to write all of my code" to "I have loops that are running. They're the ones that are prompting Claude and kind of figuring out what to do. My job is to write loops."

Both OpenAI and Anthropic have embedded the slash-goal primitive into their coding tools (Codex and Claude Code), allowing AI to run autonomously, fix its own mistakes, and accomplish complex tasks with minimal human intervention.

**The democratization question is huge.** As someone noted on social media, the new patterns "feel both evidently the future and also somehow gate-kept." The challenge for the entire industry: how do you get everyone from chatting with AI to running AI agents at scale?

OpenAI's ChatGPT overhaul will be one answer to that question. The super app isn't just an IPO play -- it's an attempt to bring the compounding value of AI agents to everyone who still thinks of ChatGPT as a conversational assistant. And that's the most important shift in AI right now: not who's making money, but how we're actually going to use it.
